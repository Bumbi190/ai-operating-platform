# Chapter 12 — Cost, Credits and Economic Governance

<a id="section-12-1"></a>
## 12.1 Chapter Status

This chapter defines the canonical architecture, governance model, lifecycle, contracts and operational responsibilities for cost, credits, budgets, pricing, reservations, usage and financial accountability within the Omnira Intelligence Fabric.

It establishes:

- how estimated, reserved, provisional and confirmed cost remain distinct,

- how user-owned, Project-owned, Tenant-owned and Omnira-managed funding sources coexist,

- how subscriptions, credits, quotas, local compute and direct charges are represented,

- how spending authority remains separate from technical Provider access,

- how budgets are defined, allocated, reserved, consumed, released and reconciled,

- how paid retries, fallbacks, variants and speculative execution remain bounded,

- how cost is attributed to the correct Tenant, Project, Plan, Step, Execution and Provider Account,

- how currency conversion and Provider credit systems are normalised,

- how refunds, failed charges, delayed usage and disputed amounts are handled,

- how local and self-hosted execution receives honest economic treatment,

- how economic routing remains subordinate to quality, rights, privacy, security and authority,

- how users receive transparent explanations before and after spending,

- how Omnira-managed plans and future commercial margins remain governed,

- how economic learning may improve estimates without gaining authority,

- and how Stage 1 shall implement the smallest trustworthy economic control plane.

Economic governance exists to help Omnira use intelligence resources efficiently without allowing convenience, automation or Provider billing systems to become uncontrolled spending authority.

A connected Provider Account does not create permission to spend.

Available credits do not create permission to consume them.

A subscription does not create unlimited capacity.

A local Model does not create zero total cost.

A cheap resource does not create permission to lower quality, privacy, rights or safety.

A high-value request does not create unlimited budget.

This chapter is normative after formal approval.

Implementation and financial conformance must be verified independently.

<a id="section-12-2"></a>
## 12.2 Purpose of Economic Governance

The Intelligence Fabric may execute work through resources with very different economic models.

These may include:

- usage-based APIs,

- monthly subscriptions,

- prepaid credits,

- promotional credits,

- bundled Omnira capacity,

- enterprise contracts,

- free tiers,

- local Models,

- private servers,

- human-assisted services,

- marketplace products,

- and Provider interfaces whose true cost is visible only after completion.

Without one canonical economic architecture, spending would become distributed across:

- Provider dashboards,

- personal subscriptions,

- Project accounts,

- local hardware,

- Agent decisions,

- hidden retries,

- browser sessions,

- and manual workflows.

This would make it difficult to answer:

- Who paid?

- Which Project consumed the value?

- What was approved?

- What was estimated?

- What was actually charged?

- Which retries consumed cost?

- Which Provider Account was used?

- Did the result meet the Quality Contract?

- Was a cheaper eligible path available?

- Did Omnira or a Provider receive a commercial benefit?

- Can the cost be reproduced or disputed?

The economic control plane exists to make those questions answerable.

<a id="section-12-3"></a>
## 12.3 Economic Governance Is Not Accounting

The Intelligence Fabric economic architecture governs intelligence-resource spending.

It is not a complete:

- bookkeeping system,

- tax system,

- payroll system,

- invoice-payment platform,

- or corporate accounting ledger.

It may produce records consumed by those systems.

It shall not silently claim to replace them.

<a id="section-12-4"></a>
## 12.4 Economic Governance Is Not Routing

Routing compares eligible resources and may use economic evidence.

The cost system defines and verifies that evidence.

Routing may select the lowest expected total-cost Candidate under Economy.

It may not invent:

- Provider price,

- available credits,

- exchange rate,

- approved budget,

- or spending authority.

<a id="section-12-5"></a>
## 12.5 Economic Governance Is Not Authority

A Budget describes how much may be spent within a defined scope.

It does not prove that the action itself is authorised.

A request may have sufficient budget but still lack:

- publication authority,

- data-processing permission,

- commercial rights,

- consent,

- or Provider approval.

Financial permission and operational authority shall remain separate.

<a id="section-12-6"></a>
## 12.6 Economic Governance Is Not Provider Billing

Provider billing records what the Provider claims or charges.

Omnira economic governance records:

- what was approved,

- what was estimated,

- what was attempted,

- what the Provider reported,

- what Omnira attributed,

- and what remains unresolved.

Provider billing is evidence.

It is not the only source of economic truth.

<a id="section-12-7"></a>
## 12.7 Economic Governance Is Not Business Value

Cost measures resource consumption.

Business Value measures the expected or realised benefit of the work.

A low-cost Output may have low value.

A high-cost Output may be justified by:

- strategic importance,

- reuse,

- conversion,

- quality,

- risk reduction,

- or time saved.

Cost and value shall remain distinct.

<a id="section-12-8"></a>
## 12.8 Economic Control Plane

The Economic Control Plane is the collection of governed services and canonical objects responsible for:

- budgets,

- funding sources,

- pricing,

- cost estimation,

- reservations,

- usage,

- cost confirmation,

- credits,

- quotas,

- reconciliation,

- alerts,

- and reporting.

It shall interact with:

- Capability Requests,

- Plans,

- Routing,

- Provider Accounts,

- Connections,

- Executions,

- Approvals,

- Assets,

- and Evidence.

It shall not expose raw payment credentials to Agents or Models.

## 12.9 Economic Object Model

The canonical economic object model should include or reference:

- Funding Source,

- Budget,

- Budget Allocation,

- Budget Envelope,

- Budget Reservation,

- Pricing Record,

- Pricing Unit,

- Cost Estimate,

- Cost Range,

- Usage Record,

- Provider Charge,

- Cost Record,

- Cost Adjustment,

- Credit System,

- Credit Balance,

- Credit Reservation,

- Quota,

- Subscription,

- Refund Record,

- Financial Approval,

- Cost Anomaly,

- and Reconciliation Record.

<a id="section-12-10"></a>
## 12.10 Funding Source

A Funding Source identifies the economic source expected to pay for an intelligence execution.

A Funding Source may be:

- user-owned,

- Project-owned,

- Tenant-owned,

- Omnira-managed,

- customer-managed,

- subscription-included,

- prepaid credit,

- promotional credit,

- local compute allocation,

- or external partner-funded.

Every paid Execution shall identify a Funding Source or explicitly unresolved funding state.

<a id="section-12-11"></a>
## 12.11 Funding Source Identity

Every persistent Funding Source shall have stable identity.

Its identity shall remain separate from:

- Provider,

- Provider Account,

- Budget,

- Credit Balance,

- and payment method.

One Funding Source may support several Provider Accounts.

One Provider Account may draw from several Funding Sources only when the Provider permits it and attribution remains clear.

<a id="section-12-12"></a>
## 12.12 Funding Source Owner

Every Funding Source shall have an explicit owner.

Possible owners include:

- human user,

- Project,

- Tenant,

- Omnira,

- customer organisation,

- or external partner.

Ownership determines who may:

- allocate,

- approve,

- suspend,

- and review spending.

<a id="section-12-13"></a>
## 12.13 User-Owned Funding Source

A User-Owned Funding Source belongs directly to the user.

Examples include:

- personal subscription,

- personal Provider credits,

- personal API billing,

- or personal local hardware.

The Fabric shall not use it silently for Project work.

Explicit permission and Project attribution are required.

<a id="section-12-14"></a>
## 12.14 Project-Owned Funding Source

A Project-Owned Funding Source exists primarily for one Project.

Its use shall remain:

- Project-scoped,

- budgeted,

- and attributable.

Familje-Stunden funds shall not be consumed for GainPilot merely because one person owns both Projects.

<a id="section-12-15"></a>
## 12.15 Tenant-Owned Funding Source

A Tenant-Owned Funding Source may support several Projects.

Project-level attribution remains mandatory.

<a id="section-12-16"></a>
## 12.16 Omnira-Managed Funding Source

An Omnira-Managed Funding Source uses capacity, credits or contracts controlled by Omnira.

It may support:

- bundled plans,

- shared Provider Accounts,

- internal compute,

- or future customer subscriptions.

It shall preserve:

- Tenant isolation,

- Project attribution,

- transparent pricing,

- and commercial-interest disclosure.

<a id="section-12-17"></a>
## 12.17 Customer-Managed Funding Source

A future customer may connect their own billing relationship.

Omnira shall preserve:

- customer ownership,

- billing scope,

- Project allocation,

- and revocation rights.

<a id="section-12-18"></a>
## 12.18 Partner-Funded Source

An external partner may fund experimentation or production.

The relationship shall identify:

- scope,

- conditions,

- expiry,

- commercial influence,

- and data implications.

Partner funding shall not silently control Provider selection.

<a id="section-12-19"></a>
## 12.19 Local Compute Allocation

Local compute may be represented through a Local Compute Funding Source or Allocation.

It may track:

- energy,

- hardware use,

- storage,

- GPU time,

- and opportunity cost.

Stage 1 may use simplified estimates.

It shall not claim that local execution has no cost merely because no Provider invoice exists.

<a id="section-12-20"></a>
## 12.20 Budget

A Budget is a governed limit on permitted economic consumption within a defined scope and period.

A Budget shall include or reference:

- budgetId,

- owner,

- Funding Source,

- currency or accounting unit,

- total limit,

- remaining amount,

- effective period,

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Environment,

- permitted Capabilities,

- permitted Providers,

- permitted cost classes,

- approval rules,

- and lifecycle.

<a id="section-12-21"></a>
## 12.21 Budget Identity

Every persistent Budget shall have stable identity.

A Budget may receive new versions or periods.

Historical Executions shall remain linked to the Budget in effect at execution time.

<a id="section-12-22"></a>
## 12.22 Budget Owner

The Budget Owner is responsible for:

- purpose,

- limit,

- scope,

- renewal,

- and policy.

The Budget Owner need not possess raw Provider billing credentials.

<a id="section-12-23"></a>
## 12.23 Budget Scope

Budget Scope may include:

- Platform,

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Production,

- Workflow,

- Plan,

- Phase,

- Step,

- Capability,

- Provider,

- Provider Account,

- Model class,

- or Environment.

Scope shall be explicit.

Missing scope shall fail closed.

<a id="section-12-24"></a>
## 12.24 Platform Budget

A Platform Budget governs Omnira-wide economic consumption.

It may support:

- registry benchmarking,

- infrastructure,

- shared evaluation,

- or managed services.

It shall not become a substitute for Project attribution.

<a id="section-12-25"></a>
## 12.25 Tenant Budget

A Tenant Budget governs aggregated Tenant spending.

Project sub-budgets may draw from it.

<a id="section-12-26"></a>
## 12.26 Project Budget

A Project Budget governs spending for one Project.

It may contain:

- monthly operating budget,

- campaign budget,

- media budget,

- research budget,

- or development budget.

<a id="section-12-27"></a>
## 12.27 Production Budget

A Production Budget applies to one bounded deliverable such as:

- Nova and Pling episode,

- The Prompt news video,

- GainPilot reel,

- or Omnira architecture audit.

Production Budgets should include retries and derivatives where authorised.

<a id="section-12-28"></a>
## 12.28 Plan Budget

A Plan Budget is the maximum amount authorised for one Plan Version.

Material Plan changes may require a new Budget Envelope or approval.

<a id="section-12-29"></a>
## 12.29 Step Budget

A Step Budget is the maximum cost one Step may consume.

It may include:

- first attempt,

- retry allowance,

- fallback allowance,

- and evaluation cost.

<a id="section-12-30"></a>
## 12.30 Capability Budget

A Capability Budget limits cost for a class of operations such as:

- video generation,

- speech synthesis,

- current search,

- or code analysis.

<a id="section-12-31"></a>
## 12.31 Provider Budget

A Provider Budget limits spending with one Provider or Provider Account.

It may reduce exposure to:

- unexpected charges,

- single-source dependence,

- or Provider pricing errors.

<a id="section-12-32"></a>
## 12.32 Environment Budget

Development, staging and production budgets shall remain separate where appropriate.

Development experiments shall not consume production capacity without explicit authority.

<a id="section-12-33"></a>
## 12.33 Periodic Budget

A Periodic Budget resets or renews on a schedule such as:

- daily,

- weekly,

- monthly,

- quarterly,

- or annually.

Reset behaviour shall be explicit.

<a id="section-12-34"></a>
## 12.34 Fixed Budget

A Fixed Budget applies to a bounded initiative and does not automatically renew.

<a id="section-12-35"></a>
## 12.35 Rolling Budget

A Rolling Budget covers a moving time window.

The calculation method shall be explicit.

<a id="section-12-36"></a>
## 12.36 Budget Version

Material changes to:

- limit,

- scope,

- owner,

- Funding Source,

- or approval rules

shall create a new Budget Version or attributable amendment.

<a id="section-12-37"></a>
## 12.37 Budget Lifecycle

Budget lifecycle states may include:

- proposed,

- awaiting approval,

- active,

- partially reserved,

- fully reserved,

- nearing limit,

- exhausted,

- suspended,

- expired,

- closed,

- and archived.

<a id="section-12-38"></a>
## 12.38 Proposed Budget

A Proposed Budget has not yet been approved for spending.

<a id="section-12-39"></a>
## 12.39 Awaiting-Approval Budget

The amount and scope are defined but authority remains incomplete.

<a id="section-12-40"></a>
## 12.40 Active Budget

An Active Budget may support eligible reservations and spending.

<a id="section-12-41"></a>
## 12.41 Partially Reserved Budget

Part of the Budget is committed to pending work.

<a id="section-12-42"></a>
## 12.42 Fully Reserved Budget

The available amount is fully reserved.

No additional spending may begin unless reservations are released or the Budget is increased.

<a id="section-12-43"></a>
## 12.43 Nearing-Limit Budget

The remaining amount has crossed a defined warning threshold.

<a id="section-12-44"></a>
## 12.44 Exhausted Budget

No additional cost may be authorised under the Budget.

Existing paid external jobs may still need reconciliation.

<a id="section-12-45"></a>
## 12.45 Suspended Budget

New reservations are blocked temporarily.

Suspension may result from:

- anomaly,

- Incident,

- review,

- or owner action.

<a id="section-12-46"></a>
## 12.46 Expired Budget

The Budget period has ended.

New work requires a new active Budget.

<a id="section-12-47"></a>
## 12.47 Closed Budget

The Budget has completed its economic lifecycle and all known charges have been reconciled.

<a id="section-12-48"></a>
## 12.48 Archived Budget

The Budget remains for history and audit.

<a id="section-12-49"></a>
## 12.49 Budget Envelope

A Budget Envelope is the request- or Plan-specific economic boundary derived from one or more Budgets and Funding Sources.

It shall include or reference:

- budgetEnvelopeId,

- parent Budgets,

- maximum authorised cost,

- expected cost,

- currency,

- Step allocations,

- retry allocation,

- fallback allocation,

- optional-work allocation,

- approval validity,

- Funding Source priority,

- and overrun behaviour.

<a id="section-12-50"></a>
## 12.50 Budget Envelope Identity

A Budget Envelope shall have stable identity and version.

Approval shall bind to the exact Envelope Version.

<a id="section-12-51"></a>
## 12.51 Maximum Authorised Cost

Maximum Authorised Cost is the hard upper boundary for the approved scope.

The Fabric shall stop before knowingly exceeding it.

<a id="section-12-52"></a>
## 12.52 Expected Cost

Expected Cost is the estimated likely total under ordinary execution.

It shall remain distinct from the maximum.

<a id="section-12-53"></a>
## 12.53 Minimum Estimated Cost

Minimum Estimated Cost represents the lowest plausible valid cost under favourable conditions.

<a id="section-12-54"></a>
## 12.54 Cost Range

A Cost Range may include:

- minimum,

- expected,

- maximum,

- and confidence.

Ranges are appropriate when Provider usage or retries are uncertain.

<a id="section-12-55"></a>
## 12.55 Budget Headroom

Budget Headroom is the difference between:

- maximum authorised cost,

- and current confirmed plus reserved cost.

Headroom may be allocated to:

- retries,

- fallback,

- or optional work.

<a id="section-12-56"></a>
## 12.56 Budget Allocation

A Budget Allocation assigns part of a Budget to a narrower scope.

It may be created for:

- Project,

- Plan,

- Step,

- Provider,

- Capability,

- or time period.

<a id="section-12-57"></a>
## 12.57 Allocation Hierarchy

Allocations may form a hierarchy such as:

Tenant Budget

→ Project Budget

→ Production Budget

→ Plan Budget

→ Step Budget

Child allocations shall not exceed available parent capacity.

<a id="section-12-58"></a>
## 12.58 Hard Allocation

A Hard Allocation cannot be exceeded without amendment or new approval.

<a id="section-12-59"></a>
## 12.59 Soft Allocation

A Soft Allocation is a planning target that may draw from shared reserve when policy permits.

<a id="section-12-60"></a>
## 12.60 Shared Reserve

A Shared Reserve supports unexpected but approved:

- retries,

- fallback,

- or corrections.

The reserve shall remain bounded.

<a id="section-12-61"></a>
## 12.61 Optional-Work Allocation

Optional variants, derivatives or enhancements should use a separate discretionary allocation where practical.

<a id="section-12-62"></a>
## 12.62 Retry Allocation

Retry budget shall remain explicit.

Retries shall not silently consume unrelated Project funds.

<a id="section-12-63"></a>
## 12.63 Fallback Allocation

Fallback budget may be reserved separately when alternative Providers have higher costs.

<a id="section-12-64"></a>
## 12.64 Human-Correction Allocation

Plans involving review or manual production may allocate expected human effort or external service cost.

<a id="section-12-65"></a>
## 12.65 Budget Priority

When several Funding Sources are eligible, the Budget Envelope may define priority.

Examples include:

1. Project-included capacity.

2. Project-owned credits.

3. Tenant shared allocation.

4. User-owned credits with permission.

5. Omnira-managed credits.

6. Paid on-demand source.

Priority remains subordinate to routing eligibility and user choice.

<a id="section-12-66"></a>
## 12.66 Funding Source Substitution

Changing Funding Source after approval may be material.

The system shall evaluate:

- owner,

- price,

- Provider Account,

- commercial relationship,

- and user expectation.

<a id="section-12-67"></a>
## 12.67 Budget Reservation

A Budget Reservation temporarily commits capacity before cost is confirmed.

It prevents several concurrent operations from overspending the same Budget.

A Reservation shall include or reference:

- reservationId,

- Budget Envelope,

- Plan,

- Step,

- selected Candidate,

- expected cost,

- maximum reservable cost,

- currency,

- expiration,

- status,

- and provenance.

<a id="section-12-68"></a>
## 12.68 Reservation Identity

Every Reservation shall have stable identity.

<a id="section-12-69"></a>
## 12.69 Reservation Purpose

The Reservation purpose shall identify the exact work being protected.

Generic permanent reservations are discouraged.

<a id="section-12-70"></a>
## 12.70 Reservation Amount

The reserved amount should reflect:

- expected Provider charge,

- retry allowance,

- minimum charge,

- and uncertainty.

It shall not exceed the Step or Plan boundary.

<a id="section-12-71"></a>
## 12.71 Reservation Currency

Reservation currency shall match the parent Budget or use an explicit conversion basis.

<a id="section-12-72"></a>
## 12.72 Reservation Lifecycle

Reservation states may include:

- proposed,

- pending,

- active,

- partially consumed,

- fully consumed,

- released,

- expired,

- cancelled,

- and disputed.

<a id="section-12-73"></a>
## 12.73 Active Reservation

An Active Reservation reduces available Budget capacity.

<a id="section-12-74"></a>
## 12.74 Partial Consumption

Part of the reserved amount has become provisional or confirmed cost.

<a id="section-12-75"></a>
## 12.75 Full Consumption

The full reserved amount has been consumed or allocated to confirmed charge.

<a id="section-12-76"></a>
## 12.76 Reservation Release

Unused reservation capacity shall be released when:

- work completes,

- work is cancelled,

- cost is lower than expected,

- or the reservation expires safely.

<a id="section-12-77"></a>
## 12.77 Reservation Expiry

Reservations shall have expiry or explicit long-running status.

Expired reservations must not disappear while active external jobs remain unresolved.

<a id="section-12-78"></a>
## 12.78 Reservation Renewal

Long-running jobs may renew reservations before expiry.

Renewal shall remain inside the Budget Envelope.

<a id="section-12-79"></a>
## 12.79 Concurrent Reservation Control

Reservation creation shall use concurrency-safe checks.

Two workers shall not reserve the same remaining budget simultaneously beyond the allowed limit.

<a id="section-12-80"></a>
## 12.80 Reservation Idempotency

Repeated Reservation requests for the same Step and attempt shall not duplicate committed capacity.

<a id="section-12-81"></a>
## 12.81 Reservation Before Paid Execution

Paid execution should normally require an active Reservation before Provider submission.

<a id="section-12-82"></a>
## 12.82 Reservation for Subscription Capacity

Even when no marginal charge is expected, a logical Reservation may protect:

- included quota,

- concurrency,

- or monthly capacity.

<a id="section-12-83"></a>
## 12.83 Reservation for Local Compute

Local jobs may reserve:

- GPU time,

- battery allowance,

- energy allocation,

- or node capacity.

This may be operational rather than financial.

<a id="section-12-84"></a>
## 12.84 Reservation for Human-Assisted Work

Manual Provider work may reserve the approved maximum spend before the operator begins.

<a id="section-12-85"></a>
## 12.85 Pricing Record

A Pricing Record represents Provider, local or managed pricing evidence.

It shall include or reference:

- pricingRecordId,

- Provider,

- Provider Account class,

- Product,

- Model or Capability,

- region,

- Pricing Unit,

- currency,

- effective time,

- expiry or review,

- source,

- confidence,

- and provenance.

<a id="section-12-86"></a>
## 12.86 Pricing Record Identity

Every material Pricing Record shall have stable identity and version.

<a id="section-12-87"></a>
## 12.87 Pricing Source

Pricing sources may include:

- official Provider price list,

- Provider API,

- contract,

- invoice,

- subscription dashboard,

- manually verified terms,

- or local cost model.

Source quality and freshness shall remain visible.

<a id="section-12-88"></a>
## 12.88 Pricing Effective Date

Pricing shall include an effective date where known.

<a id="section-12-89"></a>
## 12.89 Pricing Retrieval Date

When effective date is unknown, retrieval date shall be preserved.

<a id="section-12-90"></a>
## 12.90 Pricing Expiry

Volatile pricing may expire quickly.

Stale pricing shall not be represented as current certainty.

<a id="section-12-91"></a>
## 12.91 Pricing Confidence

Pricing confidence may be:

- exact,

- high,

- moderate,

- low,

- opaque,

- or unknown.

<a id="section-12-92"></a>
## 12.92 Pricing Unit

A Pricing Unit defines the Provider billing measure.

Examples include:

- input token,

- output token,

- cached token,

- image,

- megapixel,

- video second,

- audio second,

- character,

- request,

- job,

- compute second,

- GPU minute,

- credit,

- seat,

- storage unit,

- or subscription period.

<a id="section-12-93"></a>
## 12.93 Composite Pricing

A Provider may charge through several units.

For example:

- input tokens,

- output tokens,

- tool use,

- storage,

- and search calls.

The estimate shall include every material component.

<a id="section-12-94"></a>
## 12.94 Tiered Pricing

Pricing may change after volume thresholds.

The Pricing Record shall represent the tier logic.

<a id="section-12-95"></a>
## 12.95 Regional Pricing

Price may vary by region.

The selected Deployment or Account shall determine the applicable record.

<a id="section-12-96"></a>
## 12.96 Account-Tier Pricing

Subscription or enterprise tiers may alter:

- unit price,

- included quota,

- and rights.

Pricing shall remain Account-aware.

<a id="section-12-97"></a>
## 12.97 Promotional Pricing

Promotional prices shall include:

- expiry,

- restrictions,

- and applicable Products.

<a id="section-12-98"></a>
## 12.98 Minimum Charge

Some Provider operations impose minimum billable amounts.

The estimate shall include them.

<a id="section-12-99"></a>
## 12.99 Rounded Usage

Providers may round:

- tokens,

- seconds,

- compute,

- or storage.

Rounding rules shall be represented where material.

<a id="section-12-100"></a>
## 12.100 Failed-Request Pricing

Providers may charge for:

- rejected requests,

- partial generations,

- failed jobs,

- or moderation checks.

The system shall not assume failed means free.

<a id="section-12-101"></a>
## 12.101 Cancellation Pricing

Cancellation may result in:

- no charge,

- partial charge,

- full charge,

- or unknown charge.

The Provider-specific behaviour shall be preserved.

<a id="section-12-102"></a>
## 12.102 Retry Pricing

Every retry may create a new charge.

Retry cost shall be estimated and recorded separately.

<a id="section-12-103"></a>
## 12.103 Fallback Pricing

Fallback to another Provider or Model shall use its own Pricing Record.

<a id="section-12-104"></a>
## 12.104 Subscription

A Subscription represents recurring access purchased from a Provider or through Omnira.

It shall include or reference:

- subscriptionId,

- owner,

- Provider,

- Provider Account,

- Product,

- tier,

- billing period,

- recurring price,

- included capacity,

- overage rules,

- renewal,

- cancellation,

## 12.572 Closing Statement

Economic governance determines whether Omnira can use powerful intelligence resources without allowing technical capability to become uncontrolled cost.

The user defines what is worth doing.

The Project defines where the value belongs.

The Funding Source defines who pays.

The Budget defines how much may be consumed.

The Budget Envelope defines the bounded cost of one Plan.

The Reservation protects the limit before spending begins.

The Pricing Record defines what the resource is expected to cost.

The Cost Estimate explains the likely exposure.

The Routing Decision selects the eligible resource.

The Execution consumes measurable usage.

The Provider reports or charges its economic result.

The Cost Record attributes the result to the correct Project and Funding Source.

The Reconciliation process determines what actually happened.

The Refund or Adjustment preserves later corrections without rewriting history.

For Familje-Stunden, this architecture allows Nova and Pling productions to combine:

- local processing,

- Project subscriptions,

- user-owned creative credits,

- premium scene reserves,

- and bounded retries

without losing control of the monthly production budget.

For The Prompt, it allows research, factual verification, narration, avatar production and media composition to remain economically separate and measurable.

For GainPilot, it allows Economy routing to use inexpensive or already-included resources without pretending that low price is the only definition of value.

For Omnira, it allows:

- local Models,

- user-owned Accounts,

- Project credits,

- Tenant Budgets,

- Omnira-managed capacity,

- subscriptions,

- Provider APIs,

- and human-assisted production

to coexist within one transparent economic control plane.

The cheapest call is not always the cheapest completed result.

The most expensive Provider is not always the premium choice.

A free tier is not always commercially usable.

A subscription is not unlimited.

A local Model is not without cost.

A refund does not erase the original charge.

A connected key does not authorise spending.

A Budget does not authorise the underlying action.

Economic optimisation begins only after governance.

No paid execution without a Funding Source.

No Funding Source without ownership.

No Budget without scope.

No Reservation without purpose.

No retry without remaining allocation.

No fallback without a new economic check.

No confirmed cost without evidence.

No economic learning without boundaries.

No margin without transparency.

The user retains financial authority.

The Project retains attribution.

Omnira retains neutrality.

The Fabric converts intelligence spending into controlled, explainable and auditable value.
