# Chapter 14 — Reliability, Fallback and Recovery

<a id="section-14-1"></a>
## 14.1 Chapter Status

This chapter defines the canonical architecture, governance model, lifecycle, contracts and operational responsibilities for reliability, failure handling, fallback, recovery and continuity within the Omnira Intelligence Fabric.

It establishes:

- how reliability is defined for intelligence execution,

- how availability, durability, correctness, recoverability and continuity remain distinct,

- how failures are classified across Models, Providers, Accounts, Connections, Tools, local runtimes, human steps and internal services,

- how timeouts, retries, backoff, deduplication, idempotency and reconciliation are governed,

- how unknown outcomes remain explicit,

- how fallback preserves mandatory policy and user intent,

- how partial completion, graceful degradation and compensation are represented,

- how long-running and asynchronous work survives process failure,

- how state is recovered after crashes, outages, network partitions and Provider incidents,

- how local, external and managed resources participate in one resilience model,

- how budgets, quality, authority, privacy and rights remain binding during recovery,

- how recovery point, recovery time and continuity expectations are defined,

- how Incidents trigger containment, restoration and revalidation,

- how reliability evidence affects routing,

- how chaos, fault-injection and disaster-recovery tests are governed,

- and how Stage 1 shall implement the smallest trustworthy reliability foundation.

Reliability does not mean that every operation always succeeds.

Reliability means that the Fabric:

- knows what it attempted,

- knows what it can and cannot confirm,

- preserves durable state,

- limits repeated harm,

- recovers within governed boundaries,

- and reports outcomes truthfully.

A Provider timeout is not automatically a failure.

A retry is not automatically safe.

A fallback is not automatically equivalent.

A local Model is not automatically available.

A successful response is not automatically durable.

A completed external job is not automatically ingested.

A compensating action is not guaranteed to restore the original state.

A recovered service is not automatically ready for production traffic.

This chapter is normative after formal approval.

Implementation and operational conformance must be verified separately.

<a id="section-14-2"></a>
## 14.2 Purpose of the Reliability Architecture

The Intelligence Fabric coordinates work across systems that may fail independently.

These systems may include:

- external AI Providers,

- Provider Accounts,

- APIs,

- MCP servers,

- webhooks,

- asynchronous job queues,

- local Models,

- Runtime Nodes,

- storage,

- databases,

- secret services,

- cost services,

- policy services,

- evaluation services,

- human operators,

- publication platforms,

- repositories,

- and network boundaries.

Failure may occur:

- before a request is sent,

- while it is transmitted,

- after the Provider accepts it,

- while the Provider is processing it,

- after completion but before callback delivery,

- after Output creation but before Omnira ingestion,

- after cost is charged but before evidence is recorded,

- or after Omnira records success but before a downstream side effect is verified.

The reliability architecture exists to prevent those failures from becoming:

- duplicate spending,

- duplicate publication,

- lost Assets,

- corrupted state,

- incorrect completion claims,

- unbounded retries,

- cross-Project leakage,

- or silent operational drift.

<a id="section-14-3"></a>
## 14.3 Reliability Is Multidimensional

Reliability shall not be represented by one universal percentage.

Relevant dimensions include:

- availability,

- durability,

- consistency,

- correctness,

- recoverability,

- latency stability,

- completion probability,

- unknown-outcome rate,

- data integrity,

- idempotency,

- and operational transparency.

A Provider may have high uptime but poor callback reliability.

A Model may respond consistently while producing weak quality.

A local Runtime may be private but frequently unavailable.

A Workflow may complete often but lose evidence.

A system may be available while serving stale or incorrect state.

<a id="section-14-4"></a>
## 14.4 Availability

Availability measures whether a resource or service can accept and perform eligible work during a defined period.

Availability shall be scoped to:

- Provider,

- Product,

- Account,

- Connection,

- Deployment,

- Runtime Node,

- Capability,

- region,

- Environment,

- and time.

There is no meaningful universal available flag.

<a id="section-14-5"></a>
## 14.5 Durability

Durability measures whether accepted state survives:

- process restart,

- node restart,

- storage failure,

- service redeployment,

- or later reconciliation.

An external Provider Output is not durable inside Omnira until the required evidence and Asset content have been safely ingested or referenced.

<a id="section-14-6"></a>
## 14.6 Correctness

Correctness measures whether the system state accurately reflects what occurred.

A system that reports completed when the Provider outcome is unknown is unreliable even when no data was lost.

<a id="section-14-7"></a>
## 14.7 Recoverability

Recoverability measures whether the system can restore valid operation and state after failure.

Recovery may require:

- restart,

- replay,

- reconciliation,

- fallback,

- reauthentication,

- restoration from backup,

- human intervention,

- or controlled degradation.

<a id="section-14-8"></a>
## 14.8 Continuity

Continuity measures whether essential Project or platform functions can continue during disruption.

Continuity may use:

- alternate Provider,

- local resource,

- delayed execution,

- human-assisted path,

- or reduced service mode.

Continuity shall not weaken mandatory governance.

<a id="section-14-9"></a>
## 14.9 Resilience

Resilience is the ability to absorb disruption, preserve critical state, adapt within approved boundaries and recover without losing truth.

Resilience includes both:

- prevention,

- and recovery.

<a id="section-14-10"></a>
## 14.10 Fault Tolerance

Fault Tolerance is the ability to continue operating despite a defined component failure.

Fault tolerance may be appropriate for:

- stateless routing replicas,

- redundant storage,

- queue consumers,

- and alternate Provider paths.

Not every operation requires uninterrupted continuation.

Some high-risk operations should pause safely instead.

<a id="section-14-11"></a>
## 14.11 Graceful Degradation

Graceful Degradation provides a reduced but truthful service when full quality or capability is unavailable.

Examples include:

- draft instead of publishable final,

- still image instead of full video,

- delayed delivery instead of unsafe rush,

- local summary instead of external deep analysis,

- or human review instead of autonomous acceptance.

Degradation shall be explicitly permitted by the Completion Contract.

<a id="section-14-12"></a>
## 14.12 Reliability Is Not Success Rate Alone

A high success rate can hide severe problems such as:

- duplicate side effects,

- silent cost leakage,

- unrecoverable rare failures,

- wrong-Project execution,

- or false completion.

Reliability evidence shall include failure severity and recoverability.

<a id="section-14-13"></a>
## 14.13 Reliability Is Not Retry Count

More retries do not create more reliability automatically.

Retries may increase:

- duplicate work,

- cost,

- latency,

- Provider load,

- and side-effect risk.

<a id="section-14-14"></a>
## 14.14 Reliability Is Not Redundancy Alone

Redundant resources may share the same failure domain.

Two Model endpoints may depend on:

- the same Provider,

- region,

- account,

- network,

- or underlying Model.

Redundancy shall be evaluated by independence.

<a id="section-14-15"></a>
## 14.15 Reliability Is Not Provider Status Alone

A Provider status page may report healthy while one:

- Account,

- Model,

- region,

- quota,

- or Connection

remains unavailable.

Provider-reported state is one source of evidence.

<a id="section-14-16"></a>
## 14.16 Reliability Control Plane

The Reliability Control Plane is the governed collection of services and objects responsible for:

- failure classification,

- health,

- readiness,

- liveness,

- retries,

- backoff,

- circuit breakers,

- bulkheads,

- timeouts,

- deduplication,

- idempotency,

- reconciliation,

- fallback,

- recovery,

- continuity,

- Incident handling,

- and resilience testing.

It shall integrate with:

- Planning,

- Routing,

- Execution,

- Provider Registry,

- Model Registry,

- Credential Service,

- Cost System,

- Quality System,

- Asset System,

- Observability,

- and Incident Response.

<a id="section-14-18"></a>
## 14.18 Reliability Requirement

A Reliability Requirement defines the minimum reliability properties required for a request, Capability, Plan, Step, service or Project.

It may include:

- required availability,

- maximum unknown-outcome rate,

- durability requirement,

- retry allowance,

- recovery expectation,

- fallback requirement,

- maximum data loss,

- and maximum interruption.

<a id="section-14-19"></a>
## 14.19 Reliability Requirement Identity

Every reusable Reliability Requirement shall have stable identity and version.

<a id="section-14-20"></a>
## 14.20 Reliability Requirement Scope

Requirements may apply at:

- Platform,

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Capability,

- Workflow,

- Production,

- Plan,

- Step,

- Provider,

- or Environment level.

<a id="section-14-21"></a>
## 14.21 Platform Reliability Requirement

Platform requirements may include:

- no false completion,

- durable execution state,

- bounded retries,

- recoverable audit evidence,

- and explicit unknown outcomes.

<a id="section-14-22"></a>
## 14.22 Project Reliability Requirement

Projects may define stricter continuity needs.

For example:

- The Prompt may require current-source fallback before publication.

- Familje-Stunden may allow delayed episode generation but not corrupted canonical Assets.

- Omnira code delivery may require exact repository-state preservation.

<a id="section-14-23"></a>
## 14.23 Capability Reliability Requirement

Different Capabilities require different reliability.

Examples include:

- text draft generation may tolerate retry,

- publication requires strong idempotency,

- deletion requires verification,

- voice synthesis may tolerate alternate Provider,

- and credential rotation may require zero unresolved state.

<a id="section-14-24"></a>
## 14.24 Step Reliability Requirement

A Step may specify:

- retryable errors,

- timeout,

- fallback class,

- durability,

- and completion evidence.

<a id="section-14-25"></a>
## 14.25 Reliability Profile

A Reliability Profile is a reusable policy preset describing expected resilience and recovery behaviour.

Profiles may include:

- Best Effort,

- Standard,

- High Assurance,

- Offline Private,

- and Consequential Side Effect.

Profiles shall remain distinct from Routing Profiles.

<a id="section-14-26"></a>
## 14.26 Best-Effort Reliability Profile

Best Effort may allow:

- limited retries,

- no guaranteed fallback,

- and partial completion

for low-risk optional work.

It shall still preserve truthful state and cost.

<a id="section-14-27"></a>
## 14.27 Standard Reliability Profile

Standard may require:

- durable state,

- bounded retry,

- one eligible fallback,

- reconciliation of unknown outcomes,

- and ordinary monitoring.

<a id="section-14-28"></a>
## 14.28 High-Assurance Reliability Profile

High Assurance may require:

- independent failure domains,

- stronger durability,

- multiple recovery paths,

- stricter evaluation,

- and more frequent reconciliation.

<a id="section-14-29"></a>
## 14.29 Offline-Private Reliability Profile

Offline Private may require:

- local-only execution,

- local durable state,

- no external fallback,

- and recoverable local Node operation.

<a id="section-14-30"></a>
## 14.30 Consequential-Side-Effect Profile

Consequential side effects may require:

- precondition checks,

- idempotency key,

- exact target identity,

- verification,

- reconciliation,

- and human escalation on uncertainty.

<a id="section-14-31"></a>
## 14.31 Reliability Profile Resolution

The effective profile may derive from:

1. explicit request,

2. Capability policy,

3. Project policy,

4. Workflow Template,

5. side-effect class,

6. Environment,

7. platform default.

A lower scope may strengthen reliability.

It shall not weaken mandatory higher-level requirements without authority.

<a id="section-14-32"></a>
## 14.32 Service-Level Objective

A Service-Level Objective defines a measurable reliability target over a defined period.

It may include:

- availability,

- completion rate,

- latency percentile,

- error rate,

- recovery time,

- or durability.

An Objective is not a guarantee.

<a id="section-14-33"></a>
## 14.33 Service-Level Indicator

A Service-Level Indicator is the measured value used to evaluate an Objective.

Examples include:

- successful eligible requests,

- verified side effects,

- callback completion rate,

- or time to reconcile unknown outcomes.

<a id="section-14-34"></a>
## 14.34 Error Budget

An Error Budget represents the permitted unreliability under a Service-Level Objective.

It is an operational management tool.

It is not a financial Budget.

<a id="section-14-35"></a>
## 14.35 Error-Budget Exhaustion

When reliability Error Budget is exhausted, the system may:

- slow feature rollout,

- increase review,

- disable experimentation,

- or prioritise remediation.

<a id="section-14-36"></a>
## 14.36 Recovery-Time Objective

Recovery-Time Objective defines the target maximum time to restore an affected service or Capability after disruption.

<a id="section-14-37"></a>
## 14.37 Recovery-Point Objective

Recovery-Point Objective defines the maximum acceptable loss of durable state measured in time or events.

<a id="section-14-38"></a>
## 14.38 Maximum Tolerable Interruption

Maximum Tolerable Interruption defines the longest disruption a Project or Capability can accept before material harm occurs.

<a id="section-14-39"></a>
## 14.39 Recovery Objectives Are Scoped

Recovery objectives shall identify:

- service,

- Capability,

- Project,

- Environment,

- and failure class.

One global recovery target is insufficient.

<a id="section-14-40"></a>
## 14.40 Failure Domain

A Failure Domain is a set of resources likely to fail together.

Examples include:

- Provider,

- Provider region,

- Provider Account,

- cloud region,

- network path,

- Runtime Node,

- storage system,

- secret service,

- Adapter version,

- or human operator dependency.

<a id="section-14-41"></a>
## 14.41 Failure Domain Identity

Every material Failure Domain should have stable identity or explicit classification.

<a id="section-14-42"></a>
## 14.42 Provider Failure Domain

Several Models may share one Provider failure domain.

<a id="section-14-43"></a>
## 14.43 Account Failure Domain

One Account may fail because of:

- authentication,

- billing,

- quota,

- or suspension

while the Provider remains healthy.

<a id="section-14-44"></a>
## 14.44 Connection Failure Domain

One API, MCP or webhook Connection may fail independently.

<a id="section-14-45"></a>
## 14.45 Region Failure Domain

A regional outage may affect several Provider resources.

<a id="section-14-46"></a>
## 14.46 Runtime Node Failure Domain

Local Models on one machine share risks such as:

- power,

- storage,

- operating system,

- and hardware.

<a id="section-14-47"></a>
## 14.47 Storage Failure Domain

Several services may depend on one database or object store.

<a id="section-14-48"></a>
## 14.48 Adapter Failure Domain

A defective Adapter version may affect every Provider operation using it.

<a id="section-14-49"></a>
## 14.49 Credential Failure Domain

One revoked or compromised credential may disable several Connections.

<a id="section-14-50"></a>
## 14.50 Human Dependency Domain

A human-assisted Workflow may depend on:

- one operator,

- one device,

- one account,

- or one schedule.

That dependency shall remain visible.

<a id="section-14-51"></a>
## 14.51 Shared Failure Domain

Resources that appear redundant but share a critical dependency shall not be treated as independent fallback.

<a id="section-14-52"></a>
## 14.52 Independent Failure Domains

High-assurance fallback should prefer candidates with fewer shared failure domains.

<a id="section-14-53"></a>
## 14.53 Correlated Failure

A Correlated Failure affects several components due to one common cause.

Examples include:

- internet outage,

- Provider policy change,

- shared credential revocation,

- or common Adapter defect.

<a id="section-14-54"></a>
## 14.54 Cascading Failure

A Cascading Failure propagates from one overloaded or failed component into others.

Examples include:

- retry storm,

- queue overload,

- database exhaustion,

- or fallback overload.

<a id="section-14-55"></a>
## 14.55 Failure Containment

Failure containment prevents one failure from spreading across:

- Projects,

- Tenants,

- Providers,

- queues,

- Budgets,

- or service pools.

<a id="section-14-56"></a>
## 14.56 Bulkhead

A Bulkhead isolates capacity and failure impact.

Bulkheads may exist by:

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Provider,

- Capability,

- queue,

- Runtime Node,

- or risk class.

<a id="section-14-57"></a>
## 14.57 Project Bulkhead

One Project’s overload shall not consume all shared execution capacity.

<a id="section-14-58"></a>
## 14.58 Provider Bulkhead

Provider-specific failures should not exhaust workers for unrelated Providers.

<a id="section-14-59"></a>
## 14.59 Capability Bulkhead

High-volume image generation shall not block critical text or approval work.

<a id="section-14-60"></a>
## 14.60 Risk-Class Bulkhead

Consequential side effects may use separate workers and stricter concurrency.

<a id="section-14-61"></a>
## 14.61 Bulkhead Capacity

Bulkhead limits shall be versioned and monitored.

<a id="section-14-62"></a>
## 14.62 Bulkhead Exhaustion

When a Bulkhead is full, new work may:

- queue,

- fail fast,

- reroute,

- or degrade

according to policy.

<a id="section-14-63"></a>
## 14.63 Health

Health is a derived operational condition based on current observations.

Health shall remain distinct from:

- lifecycle,

- eligibility,

- readiness,

- and liveness.

<a id="section-14-64"></a>
## 14.64 Health Observation

A Health Observation is one time-bounded piece of evidence about a target.

It shall include:

- observationId,

- target,

- observed state,

- method,

- time,

- freshness,

- confidence,

- and provenance.

<a id="section-14-65"></a>
## 14.65 Health Observation Sources

Sources may include:

- synthetic request,

- official status page,

- production request,

- heartbeat,

- callback,

- queue depth,

- local telemetry,

- human report,

- or Provider response.

<a id="section-14-66"></a>
## 14.66 Health State

Canonical Health States may include:

- healthy,

- degraded,

- partially unavailable,

- unavailable,

- unknown,

- maintenance,

- overloaded,

- authentication required,

- quota constrained,

- and Incident active.

<a id="section-14-67"></a>
## 14.67 Healthy

Healthy means current evidence indicates ordinary operation within defined thresholds.

<a id="section-14-68"></a>
## 14.68 Degraded

Degraded means the target remains usable with reduced:

- capacity,

- latency,

- reliability,

- Feature set,

- or quality.

<a id="section-14-69"></a>
## 14.69 Partially Unavailable

Only some:

- regions,

- Models,

- operations,

- Accounts,

- or Capabilities

are unavailable.

<a id="section-14-70"></a>
## 14.70 Unavailable

The target cannot currently perform the required operation.

<a id="section-14-71"></a>
## 14.71 Unknown Health

Insufficient current evidence exists.

Unknown shall not be treated as healthy for high-consequence work.

<a id="section-14-72"></a>
## 14.72 Maintenance

Planned or declared maintenance is active.

<a id="section-14-73"></a>
## 14.73 Overloaded

The target is operational but current load exceeds safe or expected thresholds.

<a id="section-14-74"></a>
## 14.74 Health Freshness

Every health state shall declare freshness.

A stale healthy state shall not authorise consequential execution.

<a id="section-14-75"></a>
## 14.75 Health Aggregation

Aggregated health shall preserve component-level evidence.

One healthy component shall not hide another critical failure.

<a id="section-14-76"></a>
## 14.76 Readiness

Readiness determines whether a component is prepared to receive new work.

A service may be alive but not ready.

<a id="section-14-77"></a>
## 14.77 Liveness

Liveness determines whether a component is running and making progress.

A service may be ready at startup but later become stuck.

<a id="section-14-78"></a>
## 14.78 Readiness Check

Readiness may inspect:

- dependencies,

- configuration,

- credentials,

- policy access,

- storage,

- and queues.

<a id="section-14-79"></a>
## 14.79 Liveness Check

Liveness should detect:

- deadlock,

- stuck worker,

- lost lease,

- or unresponsive process.

<a id="section-14-80"></a>
## 14.80 Startup Readiness

A component shall not receive traffic before required state and dependencies are available.

<a id="section-14-81"></a>
## 14.81 Recovery Readiness

A recovered service shall pass readiness and integrity checks before production traffic resumes.

<a id="section-14-82"></a>
## 14.82 Readiness Versus Eligibility

A resource may be ready technically but ineligible under policy.

<a id="section-14-83"></a>
## 14.83 Failure Event

A Failure Event records an observed failure or abnormal condition.

It shall include or reference:

- failureEventId,

- target,

- operation,

- category,

- severity,

- time,

- error,

- retryability,

- affected scope,

- and provenance.

<a id="section-14-84"></a>
## 14.84 Error Classification

Errors shall be classified canonically.

Representative classes include:

- validation,

- policy,

- authentication,

- authorisation,

- budget,

- quota,

- rate limit,

- capacity,

- timeout,

- network,

- Provider outage,

- unsupported capability,

- quality failure,

- data integrity,

- conflict,

- dependency failure,

- cancellation,

- unknown outcome,

- and internal defect.

<a id="section-14-85"></a>
## 14.85 Validation Error

The request or Input violates schema or contract.

Validation errors are normally non-retryable without correction.

<a id="section-14-86"></a>
## 14.86 Policy Error

The operation is prohibited by policy.

Retry is inappropriate unless policy or request changes.

<a id="section-14-87"></a>
## 14.87 Authentication Error

The Credential cannot authenticate.

Recovery may require:

- refresh,

- rotation,

- or user action.

<a id="section-14-88"></a>
## 14.88 Authorisation Error

The Principal or Connection lacks permission.

Retry shall not bypass the missing authority.

<a id="section-14-89"></a>
## 14.89 Budget Error

The required cost cannot be reserved or authorised.

<a id="section-14-90"></a>
## 14.90 Quota Error

Provider or resource quota is exhausted or insufficient.

<a id="section-14-91"></a>
## 14.91 Rate-Limit Error

The resource temporarily refuses work due to request rate.

Retry may be allowed after delay.

<a id="section-14-92"></a>
## 14.92 Capacity Error

The required compute or concurrency is unavailable.

<a id="section-14-93"></a>
## 14.93 Timeout Error

An operation did not complete within the local waiting boundary.

Timeout does not prove external failure.

<a id="section-14-94"></a>
## 14.94 Network Error

Communication failed before, during or after request transmission.

The system must determine whether external execution may have begun.

<a id="section-14-95"></a>
## 14.95 Provider-Outage Error

The Provider or Product is unavailable under current evidence.

<a id="section-14-96"></a>
## 14.96 Unsupported-Capability Error

The selected resource cannot perform the requested Capability or Feature.

<a id="section-14-97"></a>
## 14.97 Quality Failure

Execution completed but the Output failed the Quality Contract.

<a id="section-14-98"></a>
## 14.98 Data-Integrity Error

Data was:

- corrupted,

- incomplete,

- mismatched,

- or unverifiable.

<a id="section-14-99"></a>
## 14.99 Conflict Error

Concurrent state changed and the attempted update no longer applies safely.

<a id="section-14-100"></a>
## 14.100 Dependency Failure

A required service, Asset, Approval, Credential or upstream Step failed.

<a id="section-14-101"></a>
## 14.101 Cancellation

The operation was intentionally stopped.

Cancellation may still leave external or partial state.

<a id="section-14-102"></a>
## 14.102 Unknown Outcome

The system cannot determine whether the operation completed, failed, or partially completed.

<a id="section-14-103"></a>
## 14.103 Internal Defect

Omnira code or configuration behaved incorrectly.

Internal defects require remediation rather than repeated Provider retry.

<a id="section-14-104"></a>
## 14.104 Error Severity

Error severity may include:

- informational,

- low,

- moderate,

- high,

- critical,

- and catastrophic.

<a id="section-14-105"></a>
## 14.105 Error Retryability

Retryability shall be determined by:

- error class,

- operation semantics,

- idempotency,

- external state,

- cost,

- deadline,

- and policy.

<a id="section-14-106"></a>
## 14.106 Retryable Error

A retryable error is one where another Attempt may succeed without unacceptable duplication or policy violation.

<a id="section-14-107"></a>
## 14.107 Conditionally Retryable Error

Retry may be allowed only after:

- reconciliation,

- delay,

- credential renewal,

- capacity change,

- or approval.

<a id="section-14-108"></a>
## 14.108 Non-Retryable Error

Retry shall not occur without changed conditions.

<a id="section-14-109"></a>
## 14.109 Retry Policy

A Retry Policy defines whether and how an operation may be attempted again.

It shall include or reference:

- retryPolicyId,

- applicable operation,

- retryable categories,

- prohibited categories,

- maximum Attempts,

- Backoff Policy,

- timeout,

- budget,

- idempotency requirement,

- reconciliation requirement,

- and fallback transition.

<a id="section-14-110"></a>
## 14.110 Retry Policy Identity

Every reusable Retry Policy shall have stable identity and version.

<a id="section-14-111"></a>
## 14.111 Retry Scope

Retry policy may apply to:

- Provider call,

- Execution,

- Step,

- Tool,

- callback,

- queue delivery,

- Evaluation,

- or side effect.

<a id="section-14-112"></a>
## 14.112 Attempt

An Attempt is one bounded execution effort under an Execution.

Every Attempt shall have:

- attemptId,

- sequence,

- selected Candidate,

- start,

- end,

- outcome,

- cost,

- error,

- and evidence.

<a id="section-14-113"></a>
## 14.113 Attempt Count

Attempt count shall include every relevant paid or consequential attempt according to policy.

<a id="section-14-114"></a>
## 14.114 Maximum Attempts

Every retryable operation shall have a finite maximum.

<a id="section-14-115"></a>
## 14.115 Retry Budget

Retry cost shall remain inside the approved Budget Envelope.

<a id="section-14-116"></a>
## 14.116 Retry Deadline

Retry shall stop when the remaining time makes success infeasible.

<a id="section-14-117"></a>
## 14.117 Retry Condition

Retry may require:

- transient failure,

- sufficient remaining budget,

- valid authority,

- unchanged Input,

- and current eligibility.

<a id="section-14-118"></a>
## 14.118 Retry Prohibition

Retry shall be prohibited for:

- policy denial,

- missing consent,

- exhausted authority,

- confirmed non-idempotent completion,

- or known permanent incompatibility.

<a id="section-14-119"></a>
## 14.119 Immediate Retry

Immediate retry should be rare.

It may be appropriate for:

- clearly transient local error,

- or atomic internal conflict

when no external side effect may have occurred.

<a id="section-14-120"></a>
## 14.120 Delayed Retry

Delayed retry is appropriate for:

- rate limit,

- temporary outage,

- capacity,

- or scheduled recovery.

<a id="section-14-121"></a>
## 14.121 Manual Retry

A human may authorise a retry after reviewing:

- outcome,

- cost,

- risk,

- and alternatives.

<a id="section-14-122"></a>
## 14.122 Automatic Retry

Automatic retry is permitted only inside the approved policy and Budget.

<a id="section-14-123"></a>
## 14.123 Retry Escalation

Repeated failure may trigger:

- stronger Provider,

- human review,

- Incident,

- or replanning.

<a id="section-14-124"></a>
## 14.124 Retry Exhaustion

When Attempts are exhausted, the system shall:

- fail,

- fallback,

- degrade,

- or escalate

according to the Plan.

<a id="section-14-125"></a>
## 14.125 Backoff Policy

A Backoff Policy defines the delay between Attempts.

It may use:

- fixed,

- linear,

- exponential,

- exponential with jitter,

- Provider-directed,

- or scheduled delay.

<a id="section-14-126"></a>
## 14.126 Fixed Backoff

A constant delay may suit predictable short disruptions.

<a id="section-14-127"></a>
## 14.127 Exponential Backoff

Delay increases after each failure to reduce load and collision.

<a id="section-14-128"></a>
## 14.128 Jitter

Jitter randomises delay to prevent synchronised retry storms.

<a id="section-14-129"></a>
## 14.129 Provider-Directed Backoff

Provider retry-after guidance may influence delay.

It shall not override Budget or deadline.

<a id="section-14-130"></a>
## 14.130 Maximum Backoff

Backoff shall have an upper bound.

<a id="section-14-131"></a>
## 14.131 Retry Storm

A Retry Storm occurs when many failed operations retry simultaneously and worsen the outage.

<a id="section-14-132"></a>
## 14.132 Retry-Storm Prevention

Controls may include:

- jitter,

- global rate limits,

- circuit breakers,

- queue shaping,

- and emergency pause.

<a id="section-14-133"></a>
## 14.133 Timeout Policy

A Timeout Policy defines how long the system waits for an operation or phase.

It shall include:

- connection timeout,

- response timeout,

- job timeout,

- callback timeout,

- reconciliation delay,

- and total Step deadline where relevant.

<a id="section-14-134"></a>
## 14.134 Connection Timeout

Connection Timeout limits time to establish transport.

<a id="section-14-135"></a>
## 14.135 Request Timeout

Request Timeout limits synchronous waiting for a response.

<a id="section-14-136"></a>
## 14.136 Job Timeout

Job Timeout limits expected duration for an asynchronous Provider job.

<a id="section-14-137"></a>
## 14.137 Callback Timeout

Callback Timeout defines when missing callback triggers polling or reconciliation.

<a id="section-14-138"></a>
## 14.138 Human-Step Timeout

Human Steps may have:

- reminder,

- escalation,

- expiry,

- or cancellation behaviour.

<a id="section-14-139"></a>
## 14.139 Total-Step Timeout

The full Step timeout includes:

- queue,

- Attempts,

- backoff,

- external processing,

- and evaluation.

<a id="section-14-140"></a>
## 14.140 Timeout Is Not Cancellation

Stopping local waiting does not prove that external work stopped.

<a id="section-14-141"></a>
## 14.141 Timeout Is Not Failure

The operation may still be running or completed externally.

<a id="section-14-142"></a>
## 14.142 Timeout Outcome

Timeout handling shall produce one of:

- confirmed failure,

- cancellation pending,

- unknown outcome,

- or continuing asynchronous state.

<a id="section-14-143"></a>
## 14.143 Idempotency

Idempotency means repeated execution with the same logical intent does not produce unintended duplicate effect.

Idempotency is essential for:

- publication,

- message sending,

- Asset creation,

- billing action,

- repository mutation,

- and external job submission.

<a id="section-14-144"></a>
## 14.144 Idempotency Key

An Idempotency Key identifies one logical operation.

It shall be:

- stable for the intended operation,

- scoped,

- collision-resistant,

- and preserved across retry.

<a id="section-14-145"></a>
## 14.145 Idempotency Scope

The scope may include:

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Capability,

- target,

- Plan Step,

- and operation version.

<a id="section-14-146"></a>
## 14.146 Provider Idempotency

Where a Provider supports idempotency keys, the Adapter should use them.

<a id="section-14-147"></a>
## 14.147 Internal Idempotency

Omnira shall enforce idempotency independently where Provider support is insufficient.

<a id="section-14-148"></a>
## 14.148 Idempotency Record

An Idempotency Record shall include:

- idempotencyKey,

- operation,

- request fingerprint,

- state,

- result reference,

- first seen,

- last seen,

- expiry,

- and provenance.

<a id="section-14-149"></a>
## 14.149 Idempotency Conflict

If the same key is reused with different material Inputs, the operation shall fail with conflict.

<a id="section-14-150"></a>
## 14.150 Idempotency Result Reuse

A duplicate request may return the existing:

- result,

- pending state,

- or unknown-outcome state

rather than creating a new effect.

<a id="section-14-151"></a>
## 14.151 Idempotency Expiry

Idempotency records require retention appropriate to the operation.

Publication and financial actions may require long retention.

<a id="section-14-152"></a>
## 14.152 Idempotency for Non-Side-Effect Generation

Generation may still require idempotency to prevent:

- duplicate charges,

- duplicate Provider jobs,

- or duplicate Asset ingestion.

<a id="section-14-153"></a>
## 14.153 Exactly-Once Illusion

Distributed systems rarely provide true universal exactly-once execution.

Omnira shall prefer:

- at-least-once delivery,

- idempotent processing,

- deduplication,

- and reconciliation

over false exactly-once claims.

<a id="section-14-154"></a>
## 14.154 Deduplication

Deduplication identifies repeated messages, callbacks, requests, Outputs or events and prevents unintended duplicate processing.

<a id="section-14-155"></a>
## 14.155 Deduplication Key

A Deduplication Key may use:

- Provider event ID,

- job ID,

- idempotency key,

- content checksum,

- correlation ID,

- or compound identity.

<a id="section-14-156"></a>
## 14.156 Deduplication Record

A Deduplication Record should include:

- key,

- source,

- first processed time,

- result,

- retention,

- and state.

<a id="section-14-157"></a>
## 14.157 Duplicate Callback

A duplicate Provider callback shall not duplicate:

- Output ingestion,

- Cost Record,

- Step completion,

- or publication.

<a id="section-14-158"></a>
## 14.158 Duplicate Queue Message

Queue consumers shall process duplicate messages idempotently.

<a id="section-14-159"></a>
## 14.159 Duplicate Output

Two identical or near-identical Outputs may still represent separate paid Attempts.

Content deduplication shall not erase economic or execution history.

<a id="section-14-160"></a>
## 14.160 Duplicate Publication

Publication shall use target-specific idempotency and verification to prevent duplicate posts.

<a id="section-14-161"></a>
## 14.161 Duplicate Asset Admission

The same Provider Asset should not become multiple canonical Assets unintentionally.

<a id="section-14-162"></a>
## 14.162 Circuit Breaker

A Circuit Breaker prevents repeated calls to a failing resource.

It may operate in states:

- closed,

- open,

- half-open,

- and disabled.

<a id="section-14-163"></a>
## 14.163 Closed Circuit

Requests may flow ordinarily.

<a id="section-14-164"></a>
## 14.164 Open Circuit

New requests fail fast or reroute because failure threshold was exceeded.

<a id="section-14-165"></a>
## 14.165 Half-Open Circuit

A limited number of test requests determine whether recovery occurred.

<a id="section-14-166"></a>
## 14.166 Circuit-Breaker Scope

Circuit breakers may apply to:

- Provider,

- Account,

- Connection,

- Model Deployment,

- Capability,

- region,

- or Adapter.

<a id="section-14-167"></a>
## 14.167 Circuit-Breaker Trigger

Triggers may include:

- failure rate,

- consecutive failures,

- timeout rate,

- unknown-outcome rate,

- or severe Incident.

<a id="section-14-168"></a>
## 14.168 Circuit-Breaker Recovery

Recovery requires fresh evidence.

Elapsed time alone shall not prove health.

<a id="section-14-169"></a>
## 14.169 Circuit-Breaker Override

A privileged operator may override the breaker for controlled testing.

The action shall be attributable.

<a id="section-14-170"></a>
## 14.170 Circuit-Breaker Misuse

A global Provider breaker shall not be opened because one Project sent invalid requests.

<a id="section-14-171"></a>
## 14.171 Load Shedding

Load Shedding rejects or delays lower-priority work to preserve critical capacity.

<a id="section-14-172"></a>
## 14.172 Load-Shedding Priority

Priority may consider:

- safety,

- user-facing latency,

- deadline,

- Project criticality,

- and side-effect consequence.

<a id="section-14-173"></a>
## 14.173 Load-Shedding Truthfulness

Rejected or delayed work shall receive explicit state.

It shall not disappear silently.

<a id="section-14-174"></a>
## 14.174 Queue

Queues may buffer work across temporary disruptions.

A queue is not a guarantee of completion.

<a id="section-14-175"></a>
## 14.175 Durable Queue

A Durable Queue preserves accepted messages across process restart and ordinary node failure.

<a id="section-14-176"></a>
## 14.176 Queue Visibility Timeout

Visibility or lease duration shall be long enough for processing and renewable where needed.

<a id="section-14-177"></a>
## 14.177 Queue Redelivery

Unacknowledged work may be redelivered.

Consumers must be idempotent.

<a id="section-14-178"></a>
## 14.178 Dead-Letter Queue

Repeatedly failing messages may enter a Dead-Letter Queue or equivalent quarantine.

The Dead-Letter Queue shall preserve:

- original message,

- failure history,

- scope,

- and remediation state.

<a id="section-14-179"></a>
## 14.179 Poison Message

A Poison Message repeatedly fails because of invalid or incompatible content.

It shall not block unrelated queue processing.

<a id="section-14-180"></a>
## 14.180 Queue Backpressure

Backpressure prevents producers from overwhelming consumers.

<a id="section-14-181"></a>
## 14.181 Queue Priority

High-priority work may use separate queues or priority classes.

<a id="section-14-182"></a>
## 14.182 Queue Isolation

Queues should be isolated by:

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Provider,

- Capability,

- or risk

where necessary to contain failure.

<a id="section-14-183"></a>
## 14.183 Queue Ordering

Ordering guarantees shall be explicit.

Global ordering should not be assumed.

<a id="section-14-184"></a>
## 14.184 Event Ordering

Late or out-of-order events shall be validated against object version and state transition rules.

<a id="section-14-185"></a>
## 14.185 Event Replay

Replaying events may restore projections or retry processing.

Replay shall not duplicate external side effects.

<a id="section-14-186"></a>
## 14.186 Replay Boundary

Replay should distinguish:

- rebuilding internal state,

- reprocessing a message,

- and re-executing an external operation.

<a id="section-14-187"></a>
## 14.187 Durable Execution State

Long-running execution state shall survive:

- process crash,

- deployment,

- worker loss,

- and temporary dependency outage.

<a id="section-14-188"></a>
## 14.188 Execution Checkpoint

A Checkpoint records safe progress that can be resumed.

It may include:

- completed Steps,

- produced Outputs,

- Provider job references,

- Budget state,

- and continuation data.

<a id="section-14-189"></a>
## 14.189 Checkpoint Identity

Every material Checkpoint shall have stable identity and version.

<a id="section-14-190"></a>
## 14.190 Checkpoint Frequency

Checkpoint frequency shall reflect:

- operation duration,

- recovery cost,

- and state mutation risk.

<a id="section-14-191"></a>
## 14.191 Safe Checkpoint

A Checkpoint is safe only when all referenced state is durable and internally consistent.

<a id="section-14-192"></a>
## 14.192 Checkpoint Resume

Resume shall verify:

- Plan Version,

- authority,

- policy,

- credentials,

- budget,

- and dependent state.

<a id="section-14-193"></a>
## 14.193 Continuation Token

A Continuation Token references resumable work without exposing secrets.

It may include:

- Execution,

- Step,

- checkpoint,

- and expected state version.

<a id="section-14-194"></a>
## 14.194 Stale Continuation

A stale Continuation Token shall not overwrite newer execution state.

<a id="section-14-195"></a>
## 14.195 Worker Lease

A Worker Lease grants temporary responsibility for processing one item.

The lease shall have:

- owner,

- expiry,

- renewal,

- and fencing or version control where needed.

<a id="section-14-196"></a>
## 14.196 Lost Lease

A worker that loses its lease shall stop committing new state.

<a id="section-14-197"></a>
## 14.197 Lease Renewal

Long-running work may renew its lease while making progress.

<a id="section-14-198"></a>
## 14.198 Zombie Worker

A Zombie Worker continues operating after its lease or authority expired.

The architecture shall prevent its late writes from corrupting current state.

<a id="section-14-199"></a>
## 14.199 Fencing

Fencing uses monotonically increasing tokens or equivalent version checks to reject stale worker actions.

<a id="section-14-200"></a>
## 14.200 Fencing Requirement

Fencing is especially important for:

- distributed locks,

- publication,

- Asset mutation,

- and long-running ownership.

<a id="section-14-201"></a>
## 14.201 Distributed Lock

Distributed locks may coordinate exclusive operations.

They shall not be treated as infallible ownership.

Lease expiry and fencing remain required.

<a id="section-14-202"></a>
## 14.202 Lock Scope

Locks shall be as narrow as practical.

Global locks create unnecessary failure coupling.

<a id="section-14-203"></a>
## 14.203 Lock Timeout

Locks shall expire or be recoverable.

Permanent orphaned locks are prohibited.

<a id="section-14-204"></a>
## 14.204 Concurrency Control

Concurrent state mutation shall use:

- optimistic concurrency,

- version checks,

- transaction boundaries,

- or equivalent controls.

<a id="section-14-205"></a>
## 14.205 Optimistic Concurrency

A write succeeds only when the expected object version remains current.

<a id="section-14-206"></a>
## 14.206 Conflict Recovery

On conflict, the system may:

- reload,

- merge safely,

- retry boundedly,

- or require human resolution.

<a id="section-14-207"></a>
## 14.207 Last-Write-Wins Prohibition

Last-write-wins shall not be used for consequential state when it can erase valid updates.

<a id="section-14-208"></a>
## 14.208 Transaction Boundary

A Transaction Boundary defines which state changes must commit atomically or be recoverable as one unit.

<a id="section-14-209"></a>
## 14.209 External Transaction Limitation

Omnira cannot assume atomic transactions across external Providers.

It shall use:

- sagas,

- idempotency,

- compensation,

- and reconciliation.

<a id="section-14-210"></a>
## 14.210 Saga

A Saga coordinates a sequence of local transactions and external side effects with explicit recovery paths.

<a id="section-14-211"></a>
## 14.211 Orchestrated Saga

A central orchestrator determines the next step and compensation.

<a id="section-14-212"></a>
## 14.212 Choreographed Saga

Services react to events.

Choreography requires strong event contracts and observability.

<a id="section-14-213"></a>
## 14.213 Saga State

Saga state shall be durable and versioned.

<a id="section-14-214"></a>
## 14.214 Saga Compensation

Compensation attempts to reverse or mitigate prior effects.

It is not guaranteed rollback.

<a id="section-14-215"></a>
## 14.215 Compensation Plan

A Compensation Plan defines:

- trigger,

- target effect,

- required authority,

- compensating action,

- verification,

- residual risk,

- and failure path.

<a id="section-14-216"></a>
## 14.216 Compensation Identity

Every material compensation operation shall have stable identity and idempotency.

<a id="section-14-217"></a>
## 14.217 Compensation Authority

Compensation may require separate authority.

Deleting a published Asset to correct a failed Workflow is still a consequential action.

<a id="section-14-218"></a>
## 14.218 Compensation Failure

Failed compensation shall create:

- unresolved state,

- Incident,

- and human escalation where needed.

<a id="section-14-219"></a>
## 14.219 Irreversible Side Effect

Some actions cannot be fully reversed.

Examples include:

- public disclosure,

- sent message,

- consumed credit,

- external model training exposure,

- or deleted data without backup.

The Plan shall identify irreversibility before execution.

<a id="section-14-220"></a>
## 14.220 Mitigation

When reversal is impossible, mitigation may include:

- correction,

- retraction,

- replacement,

- notification,

- access revocation,

- or follow-up action.

<a id="section-14-221"></a>
## 14.221 Unknown Outcome

Unknown Outcome is a first-class canonical state.

It shall be used when available evidence cannot confirm:

- success,

- failure,

- partial completion,

- or cost.

<a id="section-14-222"></a>
## 14.222 Unknown-Outcome Causes

Causes may include:

- timeout after request submission,

- lost callback,

- connection drop,

- worker crash,

- Provider status unavailable,

- missing billing evidence,

- or partial external state.

<a id="section-14-223"></a>
## 14.223 Unknown Is Not Failed

The system shall not mark unknown as failed merely to simplify state.

<a id="section-14-224"></a>
## 14.224 Unknown Is Not Successful

The system shall not claim completion without evidence.

<a id="section-14-225"></a>
## 14.225 Unknown-Outcome Record

An Unknown-Outcome Record should include:

- operation,

- last confirmed state,

- external identifiers,

- possible effects,

- cost exposure,

- reconciliation actions,

- deadline,

- and owner.

<a id="section-14-226"></a>
## 14.226 Unknown-Outcome Containment

Containment may include:

- block duplicate retry,

- reserve Budget,

- pause downstream Steps,

- and prevent conflicting side effects.

<a id="section-14-227"></a>
## 14.227 Unknown-Outcome Reconciliation

Reconciliation may inspect:

- Provider job status,

- Provider Asset library,

- callback logs,

- cost balance,

- publication target,

- repository state,

- or external message state.

<a id="section-14-228"></a>
## 14.228 Unknown-Outcome Expiry

Unknown state shall not disappear because time passed.

It may transition to:

- confirmed success,

- confirmed failure,

- unresolved Incident,

- or administratively closed with evidence.

<a id="section-14-229"></a>
## 14.229 Manual Unknown Resolution

A human may inspect external systems.

The manual result shall be attributable and evidenced.

<a id="section-14-230"></a>
## 14.230 Unknown Cost

Possible cost exposure shall remain reserved or provisional until reconciled.

<a id="section-14-231"></a>
## 14.231 Unknown Side Effect

Consequential side effects require especially strong reconciliation before retry.

<a id="section-14-232"></a>
## 14.232 Reconciliation

Reconciliation compares internal state with external reality.

It is central to reliable distributed execution.

<a id="section-14-233"></a>
## 14.233 Reconciliation Target

Targets may include:

- Provider job,

- Provider Asset,

- publication,

- repository mutation,

- Cost Record,

- credential state,

- or queue state.

<a id="section-14-234"></a>
## 14.234 Reconciliation Record

A Reconciliation Record shall include:

- reconciliationId,

- target,

- expected state,

- observed state,

- sources,

- discrepancies,

- resolution,

- actor,

- and time.

<a id="section-14-235"></a>
## 14.235 Automatic Reconciliation

Automatic reconciliation may use:

- polling,

- API lookup,

- asset listing,

- status endpoint,

- or event comparison.

<a id="section-14-236"></a>
## 14.236 Manual Reconciliation

Manual reconciliation is valid when automation is unavailable.

It shall remain clearly labelled.

<a id="section-14-237"></a>
## 14.237 Periodic Reconciliation

Long-running or high-value operations may be reconciled periodically.

<a id="section-14-238"></a>
## 14.238 Startup Reconciliation

After service restart, the system should inspect:

- running Executions,

- expired leases,

- active Reservations,

- pending callbacks,

- and unknown outcomes.

<a id="section-14-239"></a>
## 14.239 Provider Reconciliation

Provider reconciliation compares:

- submitted jobs,

- known statuses,

- returned Outputs,

- charges,

- and callbacks.

<a id="section-14-240"></a>
## 14.240 Publication Reconciliation

Publication reconciliation verifies:

- target account,

- external post identifier,

- content,

- timestamp,

- and visibility.

<a id="section-14-241"></a>
## 14.241 Repository Reconciliation

Repository reconciliation verifies:

- branch,

- commit,

- remote state,

- pull request,

- and merge state.

<a id="section-14-242"></a>
## 14.242 Asset Reconciliation

Asset reconciliation verifies:

- expected file,

- checksum,

- metadata,

- Provider reference,

- and canonical storage.

<a id="section-14-243"></a>
## 14.243 Credential Reconciliation

Credential reconciliation verifies:

- active Provider-side grants,

- revoked credentials,

- and local references.

<a id="section-14-244"></a>
## 14.244 Reconciliation Conflict

When internal and external state conflict, the system shall preserve both observations until resolved.

<a id="section-14-245"></a>
## 14.245 Reconciliation Authority

Reconciliation may read state broadly.

Corrective mutation requires separate authority.

<a id="section-14-246"></a>
## 14.246 Fallback

Fallback selects an alternate eligible path after a primary path becomes unavailable, fails, or cannot satisfy the Quality Contract.

Fallback shall preserve:

- Capability meaning,

- authority,

- data classification,

- rights,

- consent,

- budget,

- quality floor,

- and Project scope.

<a id="section-14-247"></a>
## 14.247 Fallback Is Not Retry

Retry attempts the same or equivalent path again.

Fallback changes one or more material resources or execution methods.

<a id="section-14-248"></a>
## 14.248 Fallback Is Not Replanning

Fallback remains inside the existing approved Plan when the alternative path is already defined and materially equivalent.

A new Capability path or changed Completion Contract requires replanning.

<a id="section-14-249"></a>
## 14.249 Fallback Policy

A Fallback Policy shall define:

- triggers,

- permitted candidates,

- order,

- maximum depth,

- quality implications,

- cost limits,

- approval requirements,

- and no-fallback conditions.

<a id="section-14-250"></a>
## 14.250 Fallback Trigger

Triggers may include:

- Provider outage,

- Account exhaustion,

- credential failure,

- quality failure,

- deadline risk,

- capacity loss,

- or Model retirement.

<a id="section-14-251"></a>
## 14.251 Technical Fallback

Technical fallback addresses infrastructure or transport failure.

<a id="section-14-252"></a>
## 14.252 Quality Fallback

Quality fallback changes resource after Output rejection.

<a id="section-14-253"></a>
## 14.253 Economic Fallback

Economic fallback changes path when:

- budget,

- credit,

- or pricing

makes the primary path unavailable.

<a id="section-14-254"></a>
## 14.254 Privacy Fallback

Privacy fallback shall remain inside the permitted placement class.

<a id="section-14-255"></a>
## 14.255 Human-Assisted Fallback

A human-assisted process may replace unavailable automation.

It shall preserve:

- operator,

- cost,

- evidence,

- and authority.

<a id="section-14-256"></a>
## 14.256 Local Fallback

External work may fall back locally when:

- data is eligible,

- Capability and Quality Floor are met,

- and deadline permits.

<a id="section-14-257"></a>
## 14.257 External Fallback

Local work may fall back externally only when:

- classification,

- privacy,

- authority,

- and budget

permit it.

<a id="section-14-258"></a>
## 14.258 Same-Provider Fallback

Fallback may change:

- Model,

- Deployment,

- Product,

- region,

- Account,

- or protocol

within one Provider.

<a id="section-14-259"></a>
## 14.259 Cross-Provider Fallback

- processing boundary,

- Account,

- pricing,

<a id="section-14-264"></a>

<a id="section-14-265"></a>
## 14.265 Fallback Disclosure

- Provider,

- privacy,

- quality expectation,

- latency,

- or cost.

<a id="section-14-267"></a>
## 14.267 No-Fallback State

<a id="section-14-268"></a>
## 14.268 Fallback Decision

- failed or unavailable primary path,

- trigger,

- selected alternative,

- policy,

- cost impact,

- quality impact,

- approval,

- and provenance.

<a id="section-14-269"></a>
## 14.269 Fallback Revalidation

Every fallback candidate shall be revalidated at selection time.

<a id="section-14-270"></a>
## 14.270 Fallback Success

Fallback success does not erase the original failure or cost.

<a id="section-14-271"></a>
## 14.271 Fallback Exhaustion

- fail,

- degrade,

- pause,

- or escalate.

- motion comic,

- or audio story

only when permitted.

<a id="section-14-277"></a>

<a id="section-14-278"></a>

<a id="section-14-305"></a>
## 14.305 Projection Rebuild

<a id="section-14-306"></a>

<a id="section-14-307"></a>

<a id="section-14-308"></a>
## 14.308 Data Restore

- backup,

- snapshot,

- replica,

- or Provider export.

<a id="section-14-309"></a>
## 14.309 Restore Point

<a id="section-14-310"></a>
## 14.310 Restore Validation

Restored data shall be checked for:

- integrity,

- completeness,

- version,

- and scope.

<a id="section-14-311"></a>

- source,

- scope,

- time,

- retention,

- encryption,

- integrity,

- and recovery procedure.

- data change rate,

- and criticality.

<a id="section-14-314"></a>

- recent corruption,

- delayed discovery,

- and accidental deletion.

<a id="section-14-315"></a>
## 14.315 Backup Isolation

<a id="section-14-316"></a>
## 14.316 Backup Encryption

<a id="section-14-317"></a>
## 14.317 Backup Integrity

Checksums or equivalent integrity controls shall be verified.

<a id="section-14-318"></a>
## 14.318 Restore Test

Restore tests shall occur periodically for critical systems.

- credential compromise,

- Provider-wide failure,

- scope,

- and isolation.

## 14.328 Failover Trigger

<a id="section-14-329"></a>

- state version,

- Budgets,

- active Executions,

- and idempotency records.

- stability,

- reconciliation,

- and controlled transition.

- consistency,

- read-only mode,

- queueing,

- or pause

for each operation class.

<a id="section-14-336"></a>
## 14.336 Read-Only Continuity

<a id="section-14-337"></a>

- ordering,

- idempotency,

- and conflict resolution

are defined.

<a id="section-14-338"></a>
## 14.338 Conflict After Partition

Conflicting updates shall be reconciled explicitly.

They shall not be overwritten silently.

- degraded,

- read-only,

- local-only,

- manual-assisted,

- recovery,

<a id="section-14-343"></a>

External execution is disabled.

<a id="section-14-344"></a>
## 14.344 Manual-Assisted Mode

Critical work may continue through governed human steps.

Only recovery, reconciliation and essential operations are permitted.

<a id="section-14-346"></a>
## 14.346 Emergency-Stop Mode

All non-essential and consequential execution is blocked.

<a id="section-14-347"></a>
## 14.347 Continuity-Mode Transition

Transitions shall be:

- attributable,

- scoped,

- and observable.

<a id="section-14-348"></a>
## 14.348 Continuity-Mode Exit

Returning to normal requires validation of:

- state,

- dependencies,

- credentials,

- Budgets,

- and active Incidents.

- availability,

- quality,

- billing,

- callbacks,

- Assets,

- or terms.

- open circuit,

- pause new jobs,

- reroute,

- provenance,

- reroute,

according to policy.

- crash,

- partial mutation,

- or unavailable dependency.

- refresh,

- rotation,

- reauthentication,

- or Account recovery.

## 14.364 Policy-Service Failure

<a id="section-14-365"></a>
## 14.365 Cost-Service Failure

<a id="section-14-366"></a>
## 14.366 Quality-Service Failure

<a id="section-14-369"></a>
## 14.369 Database Failure

The system shall prevent new work that cannot be recorded safely.

<a id="section-14-370"></a>
## 14.370 Queue Failure

- alternate queue,

- durable local buffering,

- or pause.

- process crash,

- disk loss,

- or Node offline state.

- restart,

- model reload,

- storage validation,

- or alternate Node.

- credential revocation,

- recovery from backup,

- reassignment,

## 14.491 Applied Example — Nova and Pling Video Provider Timeout

A character-critical video job is submitted to a Provider.

The local request times out after submission.

The correct state is:

Unknown outcome.

Provider Job

Possibly accepted.

Budget Reservation

Remains active.

Scene Step

Blocked from duplicate retry.

Reconciliation actions

1. Query Provider job status.

2. inspect Provider Asset library.

3. inspect credit balance.

4. wait for delayed callback.

5. inspect Provider billing.

6. escalate to human review if unresolved.

Only after reconciliation may the system:

- ingest the completed video,

- confirm failure and retry,

- or close the operation as unresolved Incident.

<a id="section-14-617"></a>
## 14.617 Reliability Conformance Requirements

An implementation conforms to this chapter only when:

- execution state is durable,

- failures are classified,

- retries are finite,

- timeouts remain truthful,

- duplicate effects are controlled,

- unknown outcomes are first-class,

- fallback is fully governed,

- recovery preserves history,

- Provider and internal state can be reconciled,

- and continuity modes remain explicit.

A generic try-catch block is not a reliability architecture.

A second Provider is not automatically a valid fallback.

A queue is not automatically durable execution.

A backup is not recovery until restoration is tested.

## 14.618 Closing Statement

Reliability is the discipline that allows the Intelligence Fabric to remain trustworthy when the world around it is not.

Providers time out.

Callbacks disappear.

Credentials expire.

Queues redeliver.

Workers crash.

Models are retired.

Local Nodes go offline.

Budgets become exhausted.

Users cancel work.

Networks partition.

Human operators make mistakes.

External systems report success while Outputs are missing.

Charges appear before results.

Results appear after local timeouts.

The Fabric must not hide that uncertainty.

The request defines the objective.

The Plan defines the permitted execution path.

The Reliability Requirement defines the expected resilience.

The Retry Policy defines bounded repetition.

The Timeout Policy defines waiting boundaries.

The Idempotency Record prevents duplicate effects.

The Circuit Breaker limits repeated harm.

The Fallback Policy defines approved alternatives.

The Unknown-Outcome Record preserves uncertainty.

The Reconciliation process compares internal state with external reality.

The Checkpoint preserves safe progress.

The Recovery Plan restores valid operation.

The Incident preserves the complete failure and response history.

For Familje-Stunden, reliability means that one failed video scene does not destroy the entire Nova and Pling production, duplicate credits are not consumed blindly, accepted scenes remain safe and no corrupted character Asset reaches the final master.

For The Prompt, reliability means that search outages do not produce fabricated current news, missing publication callbacks do not create duplicate posts and factual Gates remain binding during disruption.

For GainPilot, reliability means that economical production may pause, reroute or degrade honestly without losing Project state or spending control.

For Omnira, reliability means that code, Providers, Models, local Nodes, queues, credentials, Budgets and human actions can fail without the platform losing truth.

Success is useful.

Failure is expected.

Uncertainty is valid.

Recovery is governed.

No retry without classification.

No fallback without eligibility.

No timeout without truthful state.

No side effect without idempotency.

No unknown outcome without reconciliation.

No recovery without evidence.

No restored service without validation.

The user retains authority.

The Project retains continuity.

Omnira retains operational truth.

The Fabric remains dependable not because failure disappears, but because failure is bounded, visible and recoverable.
