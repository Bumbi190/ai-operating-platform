# Chapter 21 — Assets, Provenance, Rights and Consent

<a id="section-21-1"></a>
## 21.1 Chapter Status

This chapter defines the canonical architecture, object model, lifecycle, governance boundaries, evidence requirements and conformance rules for Assets, provenance, ownership, custody, licensing, rights, consent, attribution, permitted use, reuse, distribution and synthetic-media disclosure within the Omnira Intelligence Fabric.

It establishes:

- how Outputs become governed Assets,

- how source, reference, intermediate, derivative, master, distribution and archive Assets remain distinct,

- how Asset identity remains independent from filenames, URLs, storage paths and Provider IDs,

- how Asset Versions, Renditions, Representations and Packages are modelled,

- how integrity, checksums, manifests and chain of custody are preserved,

- how Asset storage, replication, movement, retention, deletion and recovery remain governed,

- how production lineage becomes machine-readable provenance,

- how execution provenance remains distinct from legal ownership,

- how rights claims, licences, grants, restrictions, terms and policy interact,

- how Provider terms are captured at the exact time and scope of use,

- how commercial-use eligibility is determined,

- how attribution obligations remain attached to Assets and Deliverables,

- how derivative, modification, adaptation, distribution, sublicensing and training rights remain explicit,

- how public-domain, open-licensed, stock, commissioned, user-owned, Provider-generated and unknown-rights Assets remain distinguishable,

- how consent for voice, likeness, image, personal data and performance is represented,

- how consent remains purpose-, Project-, audience-, channel-, territory- and time-bounded,

- how minor and guardian consent require stronger controls,

- how consent revocation affects future and existing use,

- how identity, authority and consent remain separate,

- how possession of media does not prove permission,

- how publication readiness requires both production acceptance and rights eligibility,

- how synthetic and generated media may require disclosure or labelling,

- how watermarks, metadata, signatures and content credentials are preserved,

- how cross-Project reuse remains controlled,

- how training, fine-tuning, indexing, retrieval and memory use require separate rights analysis,

- how human and AI contributions remain attributable,

- how rights uncertainty is represented without fabrication,

- how disputed Assets are quarantined,

- how incidents involving infringement, unauthorised likeness, missing consent or broken lineage are contained,

- and how Stage 1 shall implement the smallest trustworthy Asset, provenance, rights and consent foundation.

An Output is not automatically an Asset.

An Asset is not automatically owned by the Project.

Possession is not ownership.

Ownership is not permission for every use.

A licence is not consent.

Consent is not copyright ownership.

A Provider’s technical ability to generate content does not prove that the resulting use is lawful or permitted.

A valid Credential does not prove rights.

A generated image is not automatically commercially usable.

A reference Asset is not automatically reusable outside its approved purpose.

A public URL is not proof of public-domain status.

A watermark is not the complete rights state.

A missing rights record is not permission.

An Asset accepted for internal review is not automatically eligible for publication.

A historical licence does not necessarily cover a new channel, territory or audience.

This chapter is normative after formal approval.

Execution evidence, Decision Evidence, Derived Learning and Governed Memory are expanded in Chapter 22.

Implementation and legal conformance must be verified separately.

<a id="section-21-2"></a>
## 21.2 Purpose of Asset and Rights Governance

The Intelligence Fabric may create, receive, transform, store and distribute:

- text,

- code,

- images,

- graphics,

- documents,

- audio,

- Voices,

- music,

- video,

- animations,

- datasets,

- prompts,

- templates,

- Models,

- Tool packages,

- manifests,

- captions,

- transcripts,

- and composite Deliverables.

Each item may have different:

- creators,

- owners,

- licensors,

- subjects,

- Providers,

- usage terms,

- consent requirements,

- territories,

- time limits,

- attribution duties,

- confidentiality,

- and downstream restrictions.

Without canonical governance, Omnira could:

- publish a draft rather than the accepted master,

- use a private family image as a public reference,

- reuse a Voice outside its consent scope,

- assume a generated Asset is commercially usable,

- remove required attribution,

- train on Project material without permission,

- mix Assets across Tenants,

- lose the source lineage of a final composite,

- delete the only canonical master,

- or continue using an Asset after consent revocation.

The architecture exists to make every meaningful Asset answer:

- What is this Asset?

- Which Version is it?

- Where did it come from?

- Who or what created it?

- Which Inputs and references contributed?

- Which transformations occurred?

- Which Provider, Model, Tool or human was involved?

- Who owns or claims rights?

- Which licence or grant applies?

- Which uses are permitted?

- Which uses are prohibited?

- Which attribution is required?

- Which consent supports identifiable people, Voices or performances?

- When do those permissions expire?

- Which Projects, channels, audiences and territories are covered?

- What happens if the rights or consent state changes?

<a id="section-21-3"></a>
## 21.3 Asset

An Asset is a governed, identifiable and durable unit of content, media, software, data or artefact that Omnira may store, reference, transform, evaluate, deliver or reuse.

An Asset shall have:

- stable identity,

- Version,

- ownership or custody metadata,

- classification,

- provenance,

- integrity evidence,

- lifecycle,

- and use eligibility.

<a id="section-21-4"></a>
## 21.4 Output

An Output is content or data produced by one Execution, Attempt, Provider job, Tool or human contribution.

An Output may become:

- rejected evidence,

- temporary intermediate,

- Candidate,

- or governed Asset.

Output identity and Asset identity shall remain distinct.

<a id="section-21-5"></a>
## 21.5 Output-to-Asset Admission

An Output becomes a governed Asset only after required:

- retrieval,

- integrity validation,

- type validation,

- classification,

- provenance capture,

- storage,

- rights-state assignment,

- and lifecycle assignment.

<a id="section-21-6"></a>
## 21.6 Asset Is Not File

One Asset may have several files, Renditions or storage locations.

One file may be an implementation representation of one Asset Version.

File identity shall not replace Asset identity.

<a id="section-21-7"></a>
## 21.7 Asset Is Not URL

A URL is a location or access mechanism.

URLs may:

- expire,

- redirect,

- change,

- or disappear.

Canonical Asset identity shall remain independent.

<a id="section-21-8"></a>
## 21.8 Asset Is Not Filename

Filenames are human-readable labels.

They may be changed without changing the Asset.

<a id="section-21-9"></a>
## 21.9 Asset Is Not Storage Path

Storage paths are implementation details.

Moving an Asset shall not create a new Asset identity unless the content or governed meaning changes.

<a id="section-21-10"></a>
## 21.10 Asset Is Not Provider Object

A Provider Asset ID is an external identifier.

The canonical Asset remains Omnira-owned.

<a id="section-21-11"></a>
## 21.11 Asset Is Not Ownership

Asset registration records what exists.

It does not automatically determine who legally owns it.

<a id="section-21-12"></a>
## 21.12 Asset Is Not Rights Clearance

An Asset may exist in Omnira while its rights remain:

- unresolved,

- restricted,

- disputed,

- expired,

- or prohibited.

## 21.15 Asset Identity

Every Asset shall have stable canonical identity.

<a id="section-21-16"></a>
## 21.16 Asset Version

An Asset Version represents one materially distinct state of an Asset.

Changes requiring a new Version may include:

- content,

- structure,

- media samples,

- code,

- metadata affecting meaning,

- rights state,

- or accepted production status.

<a id="section-21-17"></a>
## 21.17 Asset Version Identity

Every Asset Version shall have stable identity.

<a id="section-21-18"></a>
## 21.18 Immutable Version

A released or accepted Asset Version shall not be edited in place.

Changes shall create a new Version.

<a id="section-21-19"></a>
## 21.19 Mutable Working State

An editing application may maintain mutable working state.

That state shall not replace canonical Versioning.

<a id="section-21-20"></a>
## 21.20 Asset Revision

A Revision records the change from one Version to another.

It shall preserve:

- prior Version,

- change reason,

- actor,

- Tool,

- time,

- and impact.

<a id="section-21-21"></a>
## 21.21 Asset Fork

A Fork creates a new lineage branch from an existing Asset Version.

Examples include:

- alternate language,

- different Project adaptation,

- experimental edit,

- or channel variant.

<a id="section-21-22"></a>
## 21.22 Asset Merge

A Merge combines contributions or branches.

The resulting Version shall preserve all contributing lineage.

<a id="section-21-23"></a>
## 21.23 Asset Representation

An Asset Representation is one encoded or structured representation of the same governed content.

Examples include:

- editable source,

- rendered file,

- thumbnail,

- proxy,

- waveform,

- transcript,

- or structured metadata.

<a id="section-21-24"></a>
## 21.24 Representation Identity

Every persistent Representation shall have stable identity.

<a id="section-21-25"></a>
## 21.25 Representation Equivalence

Representations may share semantic content while differing technically.

Equivalence shall be declared, not assumed.

<a id="section-21-26"></a>
## 21.26 Asset Rendition

A Rendition is a derived representation adapted for a particular technical or distribution purpose.

Examples include:

- low-resolution preview,

- web image,

- print image,

- portrait video,

- compressed audio,

- or captioned copy.

<a id="section-21-27"></a>
## 21.27 Rendition Identity

Every Rendition shall have stable identity.

<a id="section-21-28"></a>
## 21.28 Master Representation

The Master Representation is the highest-authority technical source for future derivatives.

It may be:

- editable,

- lossless,

- high resolution,

- or otherwise canonical.

<a id="section-21-29"></a>
## 21.29 Distribution Representation

A Distribution Representation is prepared for a channel or recipient.

<a id="section-21-30"></a>
## 21.30 Proxy Representation

A Proxy is intended for review or low-cost processing.

It shall not become the master accidentally.

<a id="section-21-31"></a>
## 21.31 Thumbnail

A Thumbnail is a small visual representation for navigation.

It shall preserve its relationship to the source Asset.

<a id="section-21-32"></a>
## 21.32 Transcript Representation

A transcript may represent the spoken content of audio or video.

It is not identical to the source media.

<a id="section-21-33"></a>
## 21.33 Caption Representation

Captions include timing and accessibility information beyond plain transcript text.

<a id="section-21-34"></a>
## 21.34 Waveform Representation

A waveform supports audio navigation.

It does not replace the audio Asset.

<a id="section-21-35"></a>
## 21.35 Structured Representation

Structured metadata may represent:

- scenes,

- tracks,

- pages,

- segments,

- or annotations.

## 21.38 Asset Extension

## 21.39 Asset Schema

## 21.40 Asset Language

<a id="section-21-41"></a>
## 21.41 Asset Audience

Audience restrictions may affect:

- child suitability,

- internal use,

- public use,

- or regulated distribution.

<a id="section-21-42"></a>
## 21.42 Asset Classification

Every Asset shall inherit or receive a Data Classification.

Classification may be:

- Public,

- Project Internal,

- Confidential,

- Local Only,

- or Prohibited.

<a id="section-21-43"></a>
## 21.43 Classification Inheritance

A derivative shall inherit the strongest relevant classification unless policy explicitly permits reduction after verified transformation.

<a id="section-21-44"></a>
## 21.44 Classification Reduction

Classification reduction requires:

- authorised transformation,

- validation,

- reason,

- and Decision.

<a id="section-21-45"></a>
## 21.45 Asset Owner

Asset Owner identifies the Principal or entity holding the canonical ownership relationship claimed or established inside Omnira.

Ownership claims may remain disputed or uncertain.

<a id="section-21-46"></a>
## 21.46 Asset Custodian

The Custodian is responsible for holding or protecting the Asset.

Custody does not imply ownership.

<a id="section-21-47"></a>
## 21.47 Asset Controller

The Controller determines how the Asset may be used within a defined domain.

Control may derive from:

- ownership,

- licence,

- contract,

- role,

- or policy.

<a id="section-21-48"></a>
## 21.48 Asset Operator

The Operator performs storage, transformation or delivery actions.

Operation does not imply ownership.

<a id="section-21-49"></a>
## 21.49 Asset Subject

An Asset Subject is a person, character, organisation, object, location or concept represented in the Asset.

<a id="section-21-50"></a>
## 21.50 Human Subject

Assets containing identifiable humans may require:

- privacy review,

- likeness rights,

- consent,

- and restricted use.

<a id="section-21-51"></a>
## 21.51 Minor Subject

A minor subject requires stronger guardian, privacy and publication controls.

<a id="section-21-52"></a>
## 21.52 Fictional Subject

Fictional characters may still be protected by:

- copyright,

- trademark,

- contract,

- or Project policy.

<a id="section-21-53"></a>
## 21.53 Brand Subject

Logos, marks and trade dress may require separate rights analysis.

<a id="section-21-54"></a>
## 21.54 Asset Creator

Asset Creator identifies the person, Agent, Provider, Model, Tool or combination that produced the Asset or Output.

<a id="section-21-55"></a>
## 21.55 Human Creator

A human creator shall be represented as a Human Principal or external contributor.

<a id="section-21-56"></a>
## 21.56 Agent Creator

An Agent may be attributed as an orchestrating or contributing system.

The Agent remains distinct from the Model used.

<a id="section-21-57"></a>
## 21.57 Model Contribution

A Model may be recorded as a generation resource.

It does not become the legal owner by default.

<a id="section-21-58"></a>
## 21.58 Tool Contribution

A Tool may transform or compose an Asset.

Its Version shall remain in provenance.

<a id="section-21-59"></a>
## 21.59 Provider Contribution

The Provider supplied the execution environment or service.

Provider involvement does not by itself determine ownership.

<a id="section-21-60"></a>
## 21.60 Joint Contribution

One Asset may have several human and machine contributors.

<a id="section-21-61"></a>
## 21.61 Source Asset

<a id="section-21-62"></a>
## 21.62 Source Asset Identity

## 21.63 Reference Asset

- or working document.

## 21.67 Candidate Asset

## 21.70 Distribution Asset

<a id="section-21-71"></a>
## 21.71 Archive Asset

<a id="section-21-72"></a>
## 21.72 Temporary Asset

A Temporary Asset is retained for a bounded time.

<a id="section-21-73"></a>
## 21.73 Ephemeral Output

An ephemeral Output may be discarded after validation if no policy requires retention.

<a id="section-21-74"></a>
## 21.74 Quarantined Asset

A Quarantined Asset is isolated because of:

- malware,

- unknown source,

- disputed rights,

- missing consent,

- corruption,

- or policy failure.

<a id="section-21-75"></a>
## 21.75 Rejected Asset

A Rejected Asset remains available as evidence or negative reference according to retention policy.

<a id="section-21-76"></a>
## 21.76 Superseded Asset

A Superseded Asset Version has been replaced by a newer governing Version.

<a id="section-21-77"></a>
## 21.77 Revoked Asset

- rights,

- consent,

- Incident,

- or owner Decision.

- signed content,

- time,

- and verification result.

<a id="section-21-652"></a>
## 21.652 Closing Statement

Assets are the durable things Omnira creates, receives, transforms, stores and delivers.

Provenance explains where those Assets came from.

Rights define which uses are permitted.

Consent defines which human or protected-subject uses are authorised.

Integrity proves that the recorded Version matches the stored content.

Custody explains who or what held the Asset.

Acceptance determines whether the Asset is good enough for a defined purpose.

Publication Eligibility determines whether it may be used publicly.

These are separate truths.

The Asset defines the governed object.

The Asset Version defines its immutable state.

The Representation defines its technical form.

The Location defines where it is stored.

The Integrity Record proves its content identity.

The Provenance Record preserves creation and transformation lineage.

The Chain-of-Custody Record preserves possession and transfer.

The Rights Profile summarises the permitted-use state.

The Licence defines granted rights and conditions.

The Terms Snapshot preserves Provider-specific obligations.

The Usage Grant binds permission to a Project, purpose, channel, audience, territory and time.

The Consent Grant binds human permission to an exact use.

The Guardian Authority protects minors and dependent subjects.

The Attribution Requirement preserves credit.

The Disclosure Requirement preserves transparency.

The Rights Decision evaluates one intended use.

The Publication Package contains only exact eligible Versions.

For Familje-Stunden, this architecture ensures that:

- Nova and Pling remain Project-governed character Assets,

- private family photos do not become public references,

- children’s likenesses require strong guardian authority,

- narration Voices remain inside their Consent Grants,

- music and fonts preserve their licences,

- and every public package contains only accepted and cleared material.

For The Prompt, it ensures that:

- source evidence remains distinct from generated illustration,

- screenshots and articles are not reused merely because they are online,

- synthetic visuals are disclosed when required,

- and publication Packages preserve exact source, Asset and rights lineage.

For GainPilot, it ensures that:

- exercise footage,

- performers,

- Voices,

- music,

- animations,

- logos,

- and coaching materials

remain traceable, eligible and commercially governed.

For Omnira, it ensures that:

- user uploads,

- Provider Outputs,

- local creations,

- human contributions,

- open-source packages,

- stock media,

- Models,

- Tools,

- datasets,

- and publication Deliverables

participate through one canonical Asset architecture.

The user may own the source.

The Project may hold a licence.

A Provider may generate an Output.

A human may contribute an edit.

Omnira may store the Asset.

A platform may distribute it.

A subject may grant or revoke consent.

None of those facts should be collapsed into one vague claim that the content is “ours.”

No Asset without identity.

No Version without integrity.

No derivative without lineage.

No Provider Output without terms evidence.

No commercial use without rights eligibility.

No likeness or Voice use without valid authority.

No minor media without guardian governance.

No attribution obligation left to memory.

No synthetic-media disclosure hidden when required.

No publication with unresolved mandatory rights.

No revocation without propagation.

No deletion without custody and Location review.

The user retains ownership and consent authority.

The Project retains scope and purpose.

Omnira retains provenance and enforcement.

The Fabric preserves what was created, who contributed, what may be done with it and what must never be assumed.
