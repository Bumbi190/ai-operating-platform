# Chapter 24 — Approval, Authority and Bounded Autonomy

<a id="section-24-1"></a>
## 24.1 Chapter Status

This chapter defines the canonical architecture, object model, policy hierarchy, lifecycle, enforcement boundaries and conformance requirements for approval, authority and bounded autonomy within the Omnira Intelligence Fabric.

It establishes:

- how human, Agent, Service and Device Principals receive authority,

- how authentication, identity, role, permission, authority, approval, consent and ownership remain distinct,

- how every consequential action is linked to an authorised Principal and exact scope,

- how Authority Grants remain bounded by Tenant, Project, Capability, resource, data category, Classification, action, cost, time and risk,

- how one-time approval differs from standing authority,

- how Plan approval differs from execution authorisation,

- how approval of a Budget does not approve unrelated spending,

- how approval of content does not approve publication,

- how approval of a Provider does not approve every Model, Account or data class,

- how approval of one Asset Version does not approve later revisions,

- how bounded job approval permits the Fabric to operate inside an exact envelope,

- how retries, fallbacks and replanning remain inside approved limits,

- how deviations trigger pause, denial or escalation,

- how new Providers, new Accounts, higher Budget, broader data disclosure, publication, deletion, overwrite and irreversible side effects require separate authority,

- how Authority Levels describe general operating posture without replacing explicit Grants,

- how human review, dual control, quorum, separation of duties and step-up authentication are applied,

- how delegated authority remains narrower than the delegator’s authority,

- how Agents may act autonomously only inside explicit Mandates,

- how standing Mandates remain revocable, expiring and observable,

- how approval requests present understandable scope, consequences, cost, data movement and alternatives,

- how Approval Decisions bind to exact object Versions and policy state,

- how stale approvals are invalidated,

- how approval replay and duplicate execution are prevented,

- how emergency and break-glass authority remain exceptional,

- how mobile approvals remain secure and limited,

- how revocation, suspension, expiry and Project closure propagate,

- how authority is preserved across Model and Provider changes without becoming broader,

- how approval evidence remains reconstructable,

- how denied actions remain denied despite technical capability,

- how Stage 1 shall implement the smallest trustworthy authority and approval foundation,

- and how later autonomy may increase only through evidence, policy and explicit human delegation.

Authentication is not authorisation.

Identity is not authority.

A role is not an unlimited permission set.

Access is not approval.

Approval is not consent.

Consent is not execution authority.

Ownership is not permission to ignore policy.

A successful login is not approval for a consequential action.

A Project role is not authority in another Project.

A Capability is not permission to use it.

A Provider Connection is not authority to disclose data to that Provider.

A Budget is not approval to spend it on any purpose.

A Plan is not authorised merely because it is technically valid.

A human-readable “yes” without exact scope is not sufficient evidence for high-risk execution.

Approval of a draft does not approve the final Asset automatically.

Approval of an Asset does not approve publication automatically.

Approval of publication does not prove external publication completion.

A standing Mandate is not unlimited autonomy.

A high Authority Level is not a universal override.

An Agent shall never become its own source of authority.

This chapter is normative after formal approval.

Observability, audit and Incident Response are expanded in Chapter 25.

Developer experience, testing and conformance are expanded in Chapter 26.

Implementation and operational conformance must be verified separately.

<a id="section-24-2"></a>
## 24.2 Purpose of Approval, Authority and Bounded Autonomy

The Intelligence Fabric may perform actions such as:

- reading Project data,

- summarising information,

- generating content,

- calling paid Providers,

- changing files,

- committing code,

- sending messages,

- scheduling work,

- publishing media,

- modifying external Accounts,

- purchasing credits,

- changing system settings,

- deleting Assets,

- rotating Credentials,

- moving data,

- and coordinating other Agents.

Those actions differ in:

- consequence,

- reversibility,

- cost,

- privacy,

- legal effect,

- audience,

- external visibility,

- operational risk,

- and required human judgement.

Without canonical authority governance, Omnira could:

- execute a technically valid but unauthorised Plan,

- spend beyond the user’s intended Budget,

- retry a paid action after an unknown outcome,

- publish the wrong Asset Version,

- send Confidential data to an unapproved Provider,

- allow one Agent to approve its own work,

- reuse an old approval after the content changed,

- delete files because a broad role allowed write access,

- infer standing permission from repeated past approval,

- delegate more authority than the delegator possessed,

- or continue acting after authority was revoked.

The architecture exists to ensure that every consequential action can answer:

- Who is acting?

- Which identity was authenticated?

- Which Tenant and Project are in scope?

- Which action is proposed?

- Which exact objects and Versions are affected?

- Which Capability is required?

- Which authority permits the action?

- Which policy applies?

- Which risk class applies?

- Which data will be read or disclosed?

- Which external systems, Providers and Accounts are involved?

- What will it cost?

- Is the action reversible?

- Which approval or Mandate authorises it?

- When does that authority expire?

- Which deviations require escalation?

- How can the action be stopped or revoked?

- Which evidence proves that the resulting execution remained inside scope?

<a id="section-24-3"></a>
## 24.3 Principal

A Principal is a canonical identity capable of receiving, exercising, delegating, approving or being denied authority.

Principal types may include:

- Human Principal,

- Agent Principal,

- Service Principal,

- Device Principal,

- Provider Principal,

- External Organisation Principal,

- and composite approval group.

<a id="section-24-4"></a>
## 24.4 Human Principal

A Human Principal represents an identified person.

A Human Principal may act as:

- user,

- Project owner,

- Reviewer,

- Approver,

- administrator,

- operator,

- contributor,

- or auditor.

<a id="section-24-5"></a>
## 24.5 Agent Principal

An Agent Principal represents an Omnira-owned Agent identity.

The Agent remains distinct from:

- Model,

- Provider,

- Runtime,

- Session,

- and human operator.

<a id="section-24-6"></a>
## 24.6 Service Principal

A Service Principal represents a non-human service component.

Examples include:

- Router,

- Planner,

- Scheduler,

- Adapter,

- publication Connector,

- or background worker.

<a id="section-24-7"></a>
## 24.7 Device Principal

A Device Principal represents a registered device or trusted execution Node.

Device identity may support:

- approval,

- step-up verification,

- local execution,

- or recovery.

<a id="section-24-8"></a>
## 24.8 Composite Principal

A Composite Principal represents a group whose authority requires several members.

Examples include:

- two-person approval,

- quorum,

- owner plus finance reviewer,

- or administrator plus Project owner.

<a id="section-24-9"></a>
## 24.9 Principal Identity

Every Principal shall have stable identity.

<a id="section-24-10"></a>
## 24.10 Principal State

Principal states may include:

- active,

- restricted,

- suspended,

- disabled,

- compromised,

- expired,

- and retired.

<a id="section-24-11"></a>
## 24.11 Principal State Enforcement

A suspended, disabled, compromised or expired Principal shall not exercise new authority.

<a id="section-24-12"></a>
## 24.12 Authentication

Authentication establishes that an actor controls or satisfies the required identity mechanism.

Authentication may use:

- password,

- passkey,

- hardware key,

- biometric unlock,

- signed service credential,

- device attestation,

- Session Token,

- or another approved method.

<a id="section-24-13"></a>
## 24.13 Authentication Is Not Authorisation

Authentication establishes identity confidence.

It does not determine which action is permitted.

<a id="section-24-14"></a>
## 24.14 Authentication Assurance

Authentication Assurance describes the strength of the authentication event.

Levels may include:

- low,

- standard,

- strong,

- phishing-resistant,

- device-bound,

- and hardware-backed.

<a id="section-24-15"></a>
## 24.15 Step-Up Authentication

Step-Up Authentication requires stronger assurance for a consequential action.

Examples include:

- Credential export,

- large spend,

- deletion,

- account recovery,

- Project ownership change,

- publication to a sensitive channel,

- or break-glass use.

<a id="section-24-16"></a>
## 24.16 Authentication Freshness

High-risk actions may require recent authentication.

<a id="section-24-17"></a>
## 24.17 Session Authentication

A Session carries authenticated identity for a bounded period.

Session state shall include:

- Principal,

- authentication method,

- assurance,

- issued time,

- expiry,

- device,

- and risk signals.

<a id="section-24-18"></a>
## 24.18 Session Is Not Permanent Authority

Session validity shall not extend expired or revoked Authority Grants.

<a id="section-24-19"></a>
## 24.19 Authorisation

Authorisation determines whether a Principal may perform a defined action under current policy and context.

<a id="section-24-20"></a>
## 24.20 Authority

Authority is the governed power to decide, approve, direct or execute a defined action within an explicit scope.

<a id="section-24-21"></a>
## 24.21 Permission

A Permission is one specific allowed operation.

Examples include:

- read Asset,

- create draft,

- invoke Model,

- modify file,

- publish post,

- approve Budget,

- or revoke Grant.

<a id="section-24-22"></a>
## 24.22 Permission Is Not Complete Authority

A Permission may still require:

- purpose,

- approval,

- Budget,

- data eligibility,

- policy,

- or separation of duties.

<a id="section-24-23"></a>
## 24.23 Role

A Role is a named collection of expected responsibilities and candidate Permissions.

Examples include:

- Project Owner,

- Editor,

- Reviewer,

- Publisher,

- Finance Approver,

- Agent Operator,

- Security Administrator,

- and Auditor.

<a id="section-24-24"></a>
## 24.24 Role Assignment

A Role Assignment binds a Role to a Principal in a defined scope.

It shall include or reference:

- roleAssignmentId,

- Principal,

- Role,

- Tenant,

- Project,

- environment,

- start,

- expiry,

- conditions,

- and issuer.

<a id="section-24-25"></a>
## 24.25 Role Assignment Identity

Every Role Assignment shall have stable identity.

<a id="section-24-26"></a>
## 24.26 Role Is Not Universal Authority

Role Permissions remain constrained by:

- Project,

- environment,

- policy,

- risk,

- and exact action context.

<a id="section-24-27"></a>
## 24.27 Role Inheritance

Role inheritance shall be explicit and acyclic where possible.

<a id="section-24-28"></a>
## 24.28 Role Combination

Several Roles may combine.

Conflicting restrictions shall resolve to the more restrictive result unless policy defines otherwise.

<a id="section-24-29"></a>
## 24.29 Authority Domain

An Authority Domain is a canonical area in which Decisions or actions may be authorised.

Domains may include:

- Project operations,

- finance,

- publication,

- data access,

- privacy,

- rights,

- security,

- infrastructure,

- Credential management,

- Agent configuration,

- and incident response.

<a id="section-24-30"></a>
## 24.30 Authority Domain Identity

Every Authority Domain shall have stable identity and Version.

<a id="section-24-31"></a>
## 24.31 Authority Scope

Authority Scope defines exactly where a Grant applies.

Scope dimensions may include:

- Tenant,

- Project,

- environment,

- Capability,

- action type,

- resource type,

- Provider,

- Provider Account,

- Model,

- Tool,

- data category,

- Classification,

- Asset,

- folder,

- repository,

- publication channel,

- financial account,

- device,

- geography,

- audience,

- time,

- and frequency.

<a id="section-24-32"></a>
## 24.32 Scope Intersection

Effective authority is the intersection of all applicable scope restrictions.

<a id="section-24-33"></a>
## 24.33 Missing Scope

Missing or ambiguous scope shall fail closed for consequential actions.

<a id="section-24-34"></a>
## 24.34 Authority Grant

An Authority Grant gives a Principal permission to perform or approve defined actions under explicit conditions.

It shall include or reference:

- authorityGrantId,

- grantor,

- grantee,

- Authority Domain,

- Permissions,

- scope,

- limits,

- conditions,

- effective time,

- expiry,

- delegation rights,

- revocation,

- policy Versions,

- and provenance.

<a id="section-24-35"></a>
## 24.35 Authority Grant Identity

Every Authority Grant shall have stable identity and Version.

<a id="section-24-36"></a>
## 24.36 Grantor

The Grantor must possess authority to issue the Grant.

<a id="section-24-37"></a>
## 24.37 Grantee

The Grantee may be:

- Human Principal,

- Agent Principal,

- Service Principal,

- Device Principal,

- or Composite Principal.

<a id="section-24-38"></a>
## 24.38 Grant Basis

A Grant may derive from:

- ownership,

- administrator role,

- Project role,

- contract,

- policy,

- explicit user delegation,

- or emergency authority.

<a id="section-24-39"></a>
## 24.39 Grant Validity

A Grant is valid only when:

- issuer authority remains valid,

- effective time has begun,

- expiry has not passed,

- conditions are satisfied,

- Principal is active,

- and no revocation or suspension applies.

<a id="section-24-40"></a>
## 24.40 Grant Scope Narrowing

A narrower Grant may be derived from a broader Grant.

<a id="section-24-41"></a>
## 24.41 Grant Scope Expansion

Scope expansion requires a new authorised Grant.

It shall not occur through interpretation.

<a id="section-24-42"></a>
## 24.42 Negative Grant

A Negative Grant or explicit Denial blocks a defined action despite broader Roles or Permissions.

<a id="section-24-43"></a>
## 24.43 Denial Precedence

Explicit Denial shall outrank ordinary allow Grants unless a higher-authority exception is explicitly permitted.

<a id="section-24-44"></a>
## 24.44 Grant Condition

Conditions may include:

- approval,

- Budget availability,

- specific device,

- strong authentication,

- defined time window,

- eligible Provider,

- required review,

- or successful Quality Gate.

<a id="section-24-45"></a>
## 24.45 Grant Limit

Limits may include:

- maximum cost,

- number of actions,

- duration,

- data volume,

- retry count,

- publication frequency,

- or affected objects.

<a id="section-24-46"></a>
## 24.46 Grant Expiry

Every standing Grant should have an expiry or review date.

<a id="section-24-47"></a>
## 24.47 Grant Suspension

Suspension temporarily prevents use without deleting the historical Grant.

<a id="section-24-48"></a>
## 24.48 Grant Revocation

Revocation terminates future use of the Grant.

<a id="section-24-49"></a>
## 24.49 Grant Supersession

A new Grant may supersede an earlier one while preserving history.

<a id="section-24-50"></a>
## 24.50 Grant Propagation

Revocation, expiry or restriction shall propagate to:

- active Sessions,

- approval tokens,

- queued work,

- Mandates,

- and delegated Grants.

<a id="section-24-51"></a>
## 24.51 Delegation

Delegation allows one Principal to grant a subset of its authority to another Principal.

<a id="section-24-52"></a>
## 24.52 Delegation Authority

A Principal may delegate only when the source Grant explicitly permits delegation.

<a id="section-24-53"></a>
## 24.53 Delegation Scope

Delegated scope shall be equal to or narrower than the source authority.

<a id="section-24-54"></a>
## 24.54 Delegation Depth

Maximum delegation depth shall be explicit.

<a id="section-24-55"></a>
## 24.55 Subdelegation

Subdelegation requires explicit permission.

<a id="section-24-56"></a>
## 24.56 Delegation Expiry

Delegated authority shall not outlive the source Grant.

<a id="section-24-57"></a>
## 24.57 Delegation Revocation

Revoking the source Grant shall revoke dependent delegated Grants unless policy defines a narrower transition.

<a id="section-24-58"></a>
## 24.58 Delegation Evidence

Delegation shall preserve:

- source Grant,

- grantor,

- grantee,

- scope,

- reason,

- time,

- expiry,

- and conditions.

<a id="section-24-59"></a>
## 24.59 Impersonation

Impersonation allows an authorised Principal to act through another identity context.

It shall be exceptional, visible and auditable.

<a id="section-24-60"></a>
## 24.60 Hidden Impersonation Prohibition

The acting Principal and represented Principal shall both remain visible.

<a id="section-24-61"></a>
## 24.61 Service Delegation

A human or Agent may delegate bounded execution to a Service Principal.

The service does not inherit unrelated authority.

<a id="section-24-62"></a>
## 24.62 Agent Delegation

An Agent may delegate to another Agent only within:

- its own Grant,

- approved Plan,

- Project,

- data scope,

- and allowed delegation depth.

<a id="section-24-63"></a>
## 24.63 Agent Delegation Does Not Create Autonomy

Delegation remains subordinate to the approved Mandate and Plan.

<a id="section-24-64"></a>
## 24.64 Approval

Approval is an attributable Decision by an authorised Principal that permits a defined action, Plan, Version, Budget, scope or exception.

<a id="section-24-65"></a>
## 24.65 Approval Is Contextual

Approval shall bind to:

- exact subject,

- exact Version,

- exact scope,

- exact limits,

- and applicable policy state.

<a id="section-24-66"></a>
## 24.66 Approval Request

An Approval Request asks one or more authorised Principals to approve a defined matter.

It shall include or reference:

- approvalRequestId,

- requester,

- approver requirements,

- subject,

- subject Version,

- action,

- purpose,

- scope,

- consequences,

- reversibility,

- data disclosure,

- Providers and Accounts,

- estimated cost,

- Budget impact,

- alternatives,

- risks,

- deadline,

- expiry,

- and evidence.

<a id="section-24-67"></a>
## 24.67 Approval Request Identity

Every Approval Request shall have stable identity and Version.

<a id="section-24-68"></a>
## 24.68 Approval Subject

Approval subjects may include:

- Request,

- Plan,

- Step,

- Execution,

- Asset,

- content,

- Budget,

- Provider,

- Connection,

- Credential operation,

- publication,

- deletion,

- policy exception,

- Mandate,

- or Incident action.

<a id="section-24-69"></a>
## 24.69 Approval Subject Version

The exact approved Version shall be explicit.

<a id="section-24-70"></a>
## 24.70 Approval Request Change

Material changes shall create a new Approval Request Version.

<a id="section-24-71"></a>
## 24.71 Material Approval Change

Material changes may include:

- content,

- cost,

- Provider,

- Account,

- data disclosure,

- target,

- timing,

- audience,

- publication channel,

- or reversibility.

<a id="section-24-72"></a>
## 24.72 Approval Summary

The request shall provide a concise, understandable summary.

<a id="section-24-73"></a>
## 24.73 Approval Detail

The approver shall be able to inspect detailed:

- Plan,

- object changes,

- data flows,

- cost,

- risks,

- and evidence.

<a id="section-24-74"></a>
## 24.74 Approval Diff

When an existing object changes, the request should show the exact material diff.

<a id="section-24-75"></a>
## 24.75 Approval Consequence

The request shall explain what happens if approved.

<a id="section-24-76"></a>
## 24.76 Denial Consequence

The request should explain what work will stop, defer or change if denied.

<a id="section-24-77"></a>
## 24.77 Approval Alternative

Material alternatives should be shown where practical.

Examples include:

- cheaper Provider,

- local execution,

- draft-only mode,

- reduced scope,

- delayed execution,

- or manual operation.

<a id="section-24-78"></a>
## 24.78 Approval Risk Summary

Risk should identify:

- privacy,

- security,

- financial,

- publication,

- rights,

- reliability,

- and reversibility.

<a id="section-24-79"></a>
## 24.79 Approval Data Disclosure

The request shall identify which data will be disclosed to which systems or people.

<a id="section-24-80"></a>
## 24.80 Approval Cost

Estimated cost shall include:

- initial execution,

- candidate count,

- retries,

- fallback,

- storage,

- and human review

where material.

<a id="section-24-81"></a>
## 24.81 Approval Time Window

Approval validity shall include start and expiry.

<a id="section-24-82"></a>
## 24.82 Approval Deadline

A request may expire if not decided by the required time.

<a id="section-24-83"></a>
## 24.83 Approval Decision

An Approval Decision records the authorised response to an Approval Request.

It shall include or reference:

- approvalDecisionId,

- Approval Request Version,

- approver,

- authority basis,

- decision,

- approved scope,

- conditions,

- limits,

- exclusions,

- effective time,

- expiry,

- authentication assurance,

- rationale,

- and provenance.

<a id="section-24-84"></a>
## 24.84 Approval Decision Identity

Every Approval Decision shall have stable identity.

<a id="section-24-85"></a>
## 24.85 Approval Decision States

States may include:

- approved,

- approved with conditions,

- partially approved,

- denied,

- deferred,

- revision required,

- expired,

- withdrawn,

- revoked,

- and superseded.

<a id="section-24-86"></a>
## 24.86 Approved

Approved permits the exact defined action or scope.

<a id="section-24-87"></a>
## 24.87 Approved with Conditions

Conditions shall be enforceable before or during execution.

<a id="section-24-88"></a>
## 24.88 Partial Approval

Partial approval shall identify approved and denied portions explicitly.

<a id="section-24-89"></a>
## 24.89 Denied

Denied actions shall not execute.

<a id="section-24-90"></a>
## 24.90 Deferred

Deferred requests remain undecided and unauthorised.

<a id="section-24-91"></a>
## 24.91 Revision Required

The requester must submit a new or amended request.

<a id="section-24-92"></a>
## 24.92 Approval Withdrawal

The requester may withdraw a pending request.

<a id="section-24-93"></a>
## 24.93 Approval Revocation

An approver or higher authority may revoke an approval before or during execution where policy permits.

<a id="section-24-94"></a>
## 24.94 Approval Rationale

The rationale should be concise and attributable.

<a id="section-24-95"></a>
## 24.95 Approval Conditions

Conditions may include:

- maximum Budget,

- approved Provider,

- approved Account,

- no external data disclosure,

- human review before publication,

- specific Asset Version,

- retry count,

- deadline,

- or rollback plan.

<a id="section-24-96"></a>
## 24.96 Approval Exclusion

Exclusions define what remains unauthorised.

Examples include:

- no publication,

- no deletion,

- no Provider change,

- no cross-Project use,

- or no Budget increase.

<a id="section-24-97"></a>
## 24.97 Approval Evidence

Approval evidence shall preserve:

- identity,

- authority,

- authentication,

- exact request,

- exact Decision,

- time,

- conditions,

- and scope.

<a id="section-24-98"></a>
## 24.98 Approval Signature

High-risk approvals may require a cryptographic or strongly authenticated signature.

<a id="section-24-99"></a>
## 24.99 Approval Token

An Approval Token is a bounded machine-verifiable artefact representing an active Approval Decision.

It shall include or reference:

- tokenId,

- Decision,

- subject,

- scope,

- limits,

- issuedAt,

- expiry,

- nonce,

- audience,

- and signature.

<a id="section-24-100"></a>
## 24.100 Approval Token Identity

Every Approval Token shall have stable identity or unique cryptographic identity.

<a id="section-24-101"></a>
## 24.101 Approval Token Audience

The token shall be valid only for the intended service or execution audience.

<a id="section-24-102"></a>
## 24.102 Approval Token Replay Protection

Tokens shall use:

- unique nonce,

- bounded use count,

- exact subject,

- expiry,

- and consumed state.

<a id="section-24-103"></a>
## 24.103 Approval Token Is Not Transferable

Approval Tokens shall not be reused by unrelated Executions.

<a id="section-24-104"></a>
## 24.104 Approval Token Consumption

Consumption shall be recorded atomically where consequence requires it.

<a id="section-24-105"></a>
## 24.105 Approval Token Revocation

Revoked Decisions shall invalidate unconsumed Tokens.

<a id="section-24-106"></a>
## 24.106 One-Time Approval

A One-Time Approval permits one exact action or Execution.

<a id="section-24-107"></a>
## 24.107 Single-Use Approval

Single-use authority shall be consumed after successful admission of the authorised action.

<a id="section-24-108"></a>
## 24.108 Per-Step Approval

A Plan may require approval before one specific Step.

<a id="section-24-109"></a>
## 24.109 Per-Execution Approval

One Execution is approved as a whole within stated limits.

<a id="section-24-110"></a>
## 24.110 Plan Approval

Plan Approval permits execution of one exact Plan Version inside stated bounds.

<a id="section-24-111"></a>
## 24.111 Plan Approval Is Not Unlimited Replanning

Material replanning requires revalidation or new approval.

<a id="section-24-112"></a>
## 24.112 Content Approval

Content Approval confirms that one exact content or Asset Version is acceptable for the stated use.

<a id="section-24-113"></a>
## 24.113 Publication Approval

Publication Approval permits release of an exact approved Asset or Package to an exact channel and Account.

<a id="section-24-114"></a>
## 24.114 Content Approval Is Not Publication Approval

These Decisions shall remain separate.

<a id="section-24-115"></a>
## 24.115 Budget Approval

Budget Approval permits reservation or spend within a defined purpose and scope.

<a id="section-24-116"></a>
## 24.116 Budget Approval Is Not Payment Execution

<a id="section-24-132"></a>

## 24.135 Dual Control

High-risk actions may require two distinct people.

<a id="section-24-137"></a>

Different stages may require different Principals.

Examples include:

- creator and Reviewer,

- requester and Approver,

- developer and production deployer,

- or finance requester and finance Approver.

<a id="section-24-138"></a>
## 24.138 Self-Approval Restriction

A Principal shall not approve its own high-risk request where policy requires independent review.

<a id="section-24-139"></a>
## 24.139 Agent Self-Approval Prohibition

An Agent shall not approve authority expansion, policy exceptions or high-risk actions for itself.

<a id="section-24-140"></a>
## 24.140 Conflict of Interest

A known conflict may disqualify an Approver for one Decision.

<a id="section-24-141"></a>

- groupId,

- members,

- quorum,

- scope,

<a id="section-24-142"></a>
## 24.142 Approval Group Identity

<a id="section-24-143"></a>
## 24.143 Approver Eligibility

<a id="section-24-144"></a>
## 24.144 Approver Substitution

Substitute Approvers shall be explicitly configured.

<a id="section-24-145"></a>
## 24.145 Approval Escalation

An unresolved request may escalate to a higher-authority Principal or group.

<a id="section-24-146"></a>
## 24.146 Escalation Path

- trigger,

- next Approver,

- deadline,

- and preserved context.

<a id="section-24-147"></a>
## 24.147 Escalation Does Not Broaden Scope

Escalation changes Decision authority, not the proposed action scope.

<a id="section-24-148"></a>
## 24.148 Approval Notification

Notification informs eligible Approvers that a Decision is required.

Notification itself does not approve anything.

<a id="section-24-149"></a>
## 24.149 Approval Channel

- Omnira interface,

- mobile application,

- secure web interface,

- hardware device,

- or signed external workflow.

<a id="section-24-150"></a>
## 24.150 Insecure Approval Channel

Ordinary email reply, unauthenticated chat or copied link may be insufficient for high-risk approval.

<a id="section-24-151"></a>
## 24.151 Mobile Approval

Mobile approval may be used for bounded Decisions.

It shall preserve:

- Principal,

- device,

- authentication assurance,

- exact scope,

- and Decision evidence.

<a id="section-24-152"></a>
## 24.152 Mobile Approval Minimisation

The mobile interface shall show sufficient context without exposing unnecessary protected data.

<a id="section-24-153"></a>
## 24.153 Mobile Approval Step-Up

Large spend, deletion, recovery and authority changes may require stronger verification than device unlock alone.

<a id="section-24-154"></a>
## 24.154 Voice Approval

Voice may assist navigation or request preparation.

High-risk approval shall require a reliable authenticated confirmation mechanism.

<a id="section-24-155"></a>
## 24.155 Ambiguous Natural-Language Approval

Ambiguous phrases such as:

- “go ahead,”

- “do it,”

- or “looks good”

shall not authorise broader consequential scope than the displayed request.

<a id="section-24-156"></a>
## 24.156 Confirmation Binding

The system shall bind the human response to the exact visible Approval Request.

<a id="section-24-157"></a>
## 24.157 Approval Fatigue

The architecture shall reduce unnecessary prompts without hiding material decisions.

<a id="section-24-158"></a>
## 24.158 Approval Grouping

Low-risk related actions may be grouped when scope remains understandable.

<a id="section-24-159"></a>
## 24.159 Approval Decomposition

High-risk or materially different actions shall be separated.

<a id="section-24-160"></a>
## 24.160 Dark-Pattern Prohibition

Approval interfaces shall not:

- preselect risky options,

- hide cost,

- obscure data disclosure,

- pressure acceptance,

- or make denial materially confusing.

<a id="section-24-161"></a>
## 24.161 Approval Accessibility

Approval interfaces should support:

- clear language,

- readable layout,

- keyboard access,

- screen readers,

- and understandable consequences.

<a id="section-24-162"></a>
## 24.162 Approval Localisation

Translated approval text shall preserve legal and operational meaning.

<a id="section-24-163"></a>
## 24.163 Approval Explainability

The approver should understand:

- what,

- why,

- where,

- who,

- how much,

- how long,

- and what can go wrong.

<a id="section-24-164"></a>
## 24.164 Authority Level

An Authority Level describes the general operating posture permitted for a Principal or Agent.

Authority Levels do not replace explicit Grants, policy, approvals or scope.

<a id="section-24-165"></a>
## 24.165 Canonical Authority Levels

The canonical operating levels are:

- L0 — Observe,

- L1 — Recommend,

- L2 — Prepare,

- L3 — Execute Low-Risk Reversible Actions,

- L4 — Execute Approved Operational Actions Within a Mandate,

- L5 — Manage a Delegated Domain Within Strict Boundaries,

- L6 — Exceptional Sovereign or Recovery Authority.

<a id="section-24-166"></a>
## 24.166 L0 — Observe

L0 permits:

- read-only observation,

- status inspection,

- and evidence collection

within explicit data access scope.

<a id="section-24-167"></a>
## 24.167 L0 Restrictions

L0 shall not:

- modify,

- publish,

- spend,

- approve,

- delete,

- or create external side effects.

<a id="section-24-168"></a>
## 24.168 L1 — Recommend

L1 permits:

- analysis,

- recommendations,

- alternatives,

- risk summaries,

- and approval preparation.

<a id="section-24-169"></a>
## 24.169 L1 Restrictions

L1 shall not execute consequential actions.

<a id="section-24-170"></a>
## 24.170 L2 — Prepare

L2 permits creation of:

- drafts,

- Plans,

- files in non-authoritative workspace,

- proposed changes,

- publication Packages,

- and Approval Requests.

<a id="section-24-171"></a>
## 24.171 L2 Restrictions

Prepared work shall not become effective, published or externally committed without further authority.

<a id="section-24-172"></a>
## 24.172 L3 — Execute Low-Risk Reversible Actions

L3 permits explicitly approved classes of:

- reversible,

- low-cost,

- low-impact,

- and Project-scoped

actions.

<a id="section-24-173"></a>
## 24.173 L3 Examples

Examples may include:

- create draft file,

- update temporary workspace,

- run read-only test,

- produce local preview,

- or organise non-authoritative data.

<a id="section-24-174"></a>
## 24.174 L3 Exclusions

L3 does not automatically permit:

- publication,

- deletion,

- payment,

- Credential mutation,

- Account changes,

- or Confidential external disclosure.

<a id="section-24-175"></a>
## 24.175 L4 — Execute Approved Operational Actions Within a Mandate

L4 permits operational execution inside a defined Mandate.

The Mandate shall specify:

- actions,

- Projects,

- Budgets,

- Providers,

- data classes,

- side effects,

- time,

- and escalation.

<a id="section-24-176"></a>
## 24.176 L4 Example

A publishing workflow may prepare and publish approved content to one Project Account under an active publication Mandate.

<a id="section-24-177"></a>
## 24.177 L4 Restrictions

L4 shall stop when:

- content changes materially,

- target Account changes,

- Budget exceeds limit,

- approval expires,

- or policy changes.

<a id="section-24-178"></a>
## 24.178 L5 — Manage a Delegated Domain Within Strict Boundaries

L5 permits broader operational management of one defined domain.

Examples may include:

- scheduling,

- prioritisation,

- resource allocation,

- low-risk retries,

- approved Provider selection,

- and bounded Budget management.

<a id="section-24-179"></a>
## 24.179 L5 Requirements

L5 requires:

- mature policy,

- strong evidence,

- monitoring,

- incident response,

- periodic review,

- and human revocation.

<a id="section-24-180"></a>
## 24.180 L5 Restrictions

L5 does not provide universal authority across Projects or domains.

<a id="section-24-181"></a>
## 24.181 L6 — Exceptional Sovereign or Recovery Authority

L6 represents exceptional owner, platform or recovery authority.

It may permit:

- ownership transfer,

- root recovery,

- policy restoration,

- or emergency containment.

<a id="section-24-182"></a>
## 24.182 L6 Restrictions

L6 shall:

- be rare,

- strongly authenticated,

- time-bounded where possible,

- highly audited,

- and unavailable to ordinary Agents.

<a id="section-24-183"></a>
## 24.183 Authority Level Mapping

Every Agent or Principal may have different Levels in different Domains.

<a id="section-24-184"></a>
## 24.184 No Universal Level

A Principal may be L4 for Project scheduling and L1 for finance.

<a id="section-24-185"></a>
## 24.185 Level Is Not Grant

No Level alone authorises an action.

<a id="section-24-186"></a>
## 24.186 Level Change

Increasing an Authority Level requires explicit approval and evidence.

<a id="section-24-187"></a>
## 24.187 Level Downgrade

Authority may be reduced immediately for:

- Incident,

- poor performance,

- user Decision,

- policy change,

- or Project state.

<a id="section-24-188"></a>
## 24.188 Autonomy

Autonomy is the ability of an Agent or system to choose and perform actions without requiring a new human Decision for every Step.

<a id="section-24-189"></a>
## 24.189 Bounded Autonomy

Bounded Autonomy permits independent operation only inside explicit:

- scope,

- authority,

- Budget,

- policy,

- risk,

- data,

- time,

- and outcome boundaries.

<a id="section-24-190"></a>
## 24.190 Autonomy Is Not Independence

An autonomous Agent remains subordinate to:

- human authority,

- Platform policy,

- Project policy,

- and active Mandates.

<a id="section-24-191"></a>
## 24.191 Autonomy Is Not Unlimited Delegation

An Agent shall not expand its own operating envelope.

<a id="section-24-192"></a>

- autonomyEnvelopeId,

- Principal or Agent,

- Authority Domain,

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Capabilities,

- permitted actions,

- prohibited actions,

- data categories,

- Classifications,

- Providers,

- Accounts,

- Models,

- Tools,

- Budget,

- time,

- frequency,

- retry,

- fallback,

- side-effect,

- reversibility,

- Quality,

- monitoring,

- and escalation.

## 24.193 Autonomy Envelope Identity

Every Autonomy Envelope shall have stable identity and Version.

<a id="section-24-194"></a>
## 24.194 Autonomy Envelope Versioning

<a id="section-24-195"></a>
## 24.195 Autonomy Envelope Owner

Every Envelope shall have an accountable human or governance owner.

<a id="section-24-196"></a>
## 24.196 Autonomy Envelope Expiry

Every Envelope shall have expiry or mandatory review.

<a id="section-24-197"></a>
## 24.197 Autonomy Envelope Suspension

An Envelope may be suspended without deleting its history.

<a id="section-24-198"></a>
## 24.198 Mandate

A Mandate is an active Authority Grant authorising an Agent or Service to pursue a defined operational objective within an Autonomy Envelope.

<a id="section-24-199"></a>
## 24.199 Mandate Identity

Every Mandate shall have stable identity and Version.

<a id="section-24-200"></a>
## 24.200 Mandate Purpose

A Mandate shall state the objective and expected outcomes.

<a id="section-24-201"></a>
## 24.201 Mandate Scope

A Mandate shall include exact:

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Capability,

- action,

- resource,

- data,

- cost,

- time,

- and side-effect scope.

<a id="section-24-202"></a>
## 24.202 Mandate Limits

Limits shall be measurable and enforceable.

<a id="section-24-203"></a>
## 24.203 Mandate Conditions

Conditions may include:

- valid Quality Gate,

- no unresolved Incident,

- approved Provider health,

- fresh user approval,

- or Budget availability.

<a id="section-24-204"></a>
## 24.204 Mandate Exclusions

Exclusions shall identify actions that always require separate approval.

<a id="section-24-205"></a>
## 24.205 Mandatory Separate Approval

The following should require separate approval unless an explicit high-assurance Mandate states otherwise:

- new Provider,

- new Provider Account,

- higher Budget,

- publication,

- deletion,

- overwrite,

- Credential export,

- access expansion,

- policy exception,

- ownership change,

- external sharing,

- and irreversible action.

<a id="section-24-206"></a>
## 24.206 Standing Mandate

A Standing Mandate permits repeated actions within recurring boundaries.

<a id="section-24-207"></a>
## 24.207 Standing Mandate Examples

Examples may include:

- publish two approved Project posts per day,

- perform nightly read-only health checks,

- generate draft analytics reports,

- or reconcile Provider jobs hourly.

<a id="section-24-208"></a>
## 24.208 Standing Mandate Frequency

Maximum frequency and volume shall be explicit.

<a id="section-24-209"></a>
## 24.209 Standing Mandate Cost

Maximum cost per action and per period shall be explicit.

<a id="section-24-210"></a>
## 24.210 Standing Mandate Review

Standing Mandates shall receive periodic review.

<a id="section-24-211"></a>
## 24.211 Standing Mandate Revocation

The user or authorised owner shall be able to revoke the Mandate promptly.

<a id="section-24-212"></a>
## 24.212 Bounded Job Approval

Bounded Job Approval authorises one job or workflow to operate independently inside exact limits.

It shall define:

- objective,

- Plan Version,

- allowed Steps,

- Providers,

- Accounts,

- data scope,

- Budget,

- retry count,

- fallback options,

- deadline,

- and escalation triggers.

<a id="section-24-213"></a>
## 24.213 Bounded Job Operation

The Fabric may execute, retry and use eligible fallback inside the approved boundaries without requesting approval for each ordinary Step.

<a id="section-24-214"></a>
## 24.214 Bounded Job Deviation

The Fabric shall stop or escalate when:

- Plan changes materially,

- Provider changes outside the approved set,

- Budget rises,

- data disclosure broadens,

- Quality Floor changes,

- target changes,

- publication becomes required,

- deletion becomes required,

- or outcome becomes unknown beyond the approved policy.

<a id="section-24-215"></a>
## 24.215 Bounded Job Completion

Completion shall preserve evidence that all actions remained inside the approved envelope.

<a id="section-24-216"></a>

- Principal identity,

- active Grants,

- Approval Decisions,

- Mandate,

- Plan Version,

- policy,

- Budget,

- data eligibility,

- and current state.

<a id="section-24-217"></a>
## 24.217 Execution Authorisation Identity

Every material Execution Authorisation shall have stable identity.

<a id="section-24-218"></a>

Authorisation shall occur:

- before execution,

- before each consequential Step,

- and after material state change.

<a id="section-24-219"></a>
## 24.219 Authorisation Inputs

The evaluator shall consider:

- acting Principal,

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Capability,

- action,

- target,

- Version,

- data,

- Provider,

- Account,

- Budget,

- time,

- risk,

- and active Incidents.

States may include:

- authorised,

- authorised with conditions,

- denied,

- approval required,

- reauthentication required,

- policy review required,

- and indeterminate.

<a id="section-24-221"></a>

<a id="section-24-222"></a>

- Principal,

- Project,

- action,

- target,

## 24.224 Continuous Authorisation

<a id="section-24-225"></a>
## 24.225 Queued Work Authorisation

<a id="section-24-226"></a>
## 24.226 Delayed Execution

<a id="section-24-227"></a>
## 24.227 Scheduled Execution

- active Mandate,

- exact schedule,

- target state,

- content Version,

- Account,

- and Budget

at run time.

<a id="section-24-228"></a>
## 24.228 Long-Running Execution

Long-running work shall revalidate authority before:

- expensive stages,

- external side effects,

- publication,

- deletion,

- and final delivery.

<a id="section-24-229"></a>
## 24.229 Plan Change

A changed Plan shall receive new authorisation when material.

<a id="section-24-230"></a>
## 24.230 Non-Material Plan Adjustment

Minor internal scheduling or equivalent-resource adjustment may remain authorised when explicitly allowed.

<a id="section-24-231"></a>
## 24.231 Material Plan Adjustment

- new Provider,

- changed data flow,

- changed target,

- increased cost,

- increased risk,

## 24.232 Retry Authority

- error class,

- count,

- time,

- cost,

- and idempotency boundaries.

<a id="section-24-234"></a>

<a id="section-24-235"></a>
## 24.235 Retry After Unknown Outcome

Retry after unknown outcome shall require reconciliation or explicit authority.

<a id="section-24-236"></a>
## 24.236 Retry Cost

Cumulative retry cost shall count against the approved Budget.

<a id="section-24-237"></a>
## 24.237 Retry Scope

Retry shall not broaden data, Provider or action scope.

<a id="section-24-238"></a>
## 24.238 Fallback Authority

<a id="section-24-239"></a>
## 24.239 Fallback Provider Set

The approved Provider set shall be explicit.

<a id="section-24-240"></a>
## 24.240 Fallback Model Set

The approved Model or Deployment set shall be explicit where material.

<a id="section-24-241"></a>
## 24.241 Fallback Data Review

- Classification,

- privacy,

- rights,

- consent,

- retention,

- and region.

<a id="section-24-242"></a>
## 24.242 Fallback Cost Review

Fallback shall remain inside the approved Budget.

<a id="section-24-243"></a>
## 24.243 Fallback Quality Review

Fallback shall preserve the Quality Floor.

<a id="section-24-244"></a>
## 24.244 Pinned Routing Authority

Pinned routing shall not switch Providers or Models silently.

<a id="section-24-245"></a>
## 24.245 Private Routing Authority

Private routing shall not fall back to ordinary external Providers.

<a id="section-24-246"></a>
## 24.246 Replanning Authority

The Planner may replan only inside the authorised change envelope.

<a id="section-24-247"></a>

- Budget threshold,

- repeated failure,

- unknown outcome,

- policy conflict,

- low confidence,

- rights uncertainty,

- privacy uncertainty,

- or deadline risk.

- trigger,

- current state,

- required Decision,

- alternatives,

- deadline,

- and affected work.

<a id="section-24-249"></a>

## 24.250 Stop

<a id="section-24-251"></a>
## 24.251 Abort

Abort attempts immediate termination.

External work may still require reconciliation.

<a id="section-24-252"></a>
## 24.252 Human Takeover

A human may take over a workflow.

The handoff shall preserve:

- state,

- authority,

- evidence,

- and pending risks.

<a id="section-24-253"></a>
## 24.253 Human-Assisted Mandate

A Mandate may include defined manual Steps.

Manual operation shall remain truthful and attributable.

<a id="section-24-254"></a>
## 24.254 Manual Procedure Authority

A human operator receives authority only for the exact procedure and scope.

<a id="section-24-255"></a>
## 24.255 Manual Interface Does Not Broaden Authority

Operating a Provider dashboard manually shall not allow unapproved work.

<a id="section-24-256"></a>
## 24.256 Risk Class

Risk Class describes the consequence and control requirement of an action.

Canonical classes may include:

- R0 — No Material Side Effect,

- R1 — Low and Reversible,

- R2 — Moderate Operational,

- R3 — Consequential,

- R4 — High Impact,

- R5 — Critical or Sovereign.

<a id="section-24-257"></a>
## 24.257 R0 — No Material Side Effect

Examples include:

- read public metadata,

- calculate estimate,

- inspect status,

- or generate non-persistent preview.

<a id="section-24-258"></a>
## 24.258 R1 — Low and Reversible

Examples include:

- create draft,

- update temporary workspace,

- or add non-public annotation.

<a id="section-24-259"></a>
## 24.259 R2 — Moderate Operational

Examples include:

- call a paid Provider within a small Budget,

- modify Project Internal files with Version control,

- or schedule approved work.

<a id="section-24-260"></a>
## 24.260 R3 — Consequential

Examples include:

- publish content,

- send external message,

- commit code to an active branch,

- or create a customer-facing Asset.

<a id="section-24-261"></a>
## 24.261 R4 — High Impact

Examples include:

- delete canonical data,

- rotate production Credentials,

- deploy to production,

- make substantial purchase,

- or process highly sensitive data externally.

<a id="section-24-262"></a>
## 24.262 R5 — Critical or Sovereign

Examples include:

- transfer Project ownership,

- disable platform security,

- access root recovery,

- delete Tenant data,

- or override non-waivable policy.

<a id="section-24-263"></a>
## 24.263 Risk Classification

Risk shall consider:

- impact,

- reversibility,

- scope,

- data sensitivity,

- cost,

- legal effect,

- external visibility,

- and recovery difficulty.

<a id="section-24-264"></a>

## 24.736 Approval, Authority and Bounded Autonomy Invariants

The following invariants shall remain true:

- No authentication event becomes action authority.

- No Role becomes universal permission.

- No access Grant becomes approval for a consequential action.

- No approval becomes consent, rights or ownership.

- No Budget becomes unrestricted spending authority.

- No content approval becomes publication authority automatically.

- No Plan approval survives material Plan change silently.

- No Approval Decision applies to an unspecified Version.

- No Approval Token may be replayed or transferred.

- No Agent approves its own authority expansion.

- No delegated authority exceeds the source Grant.

- No Authority Level overrides explicit Denial or policy.

- No standing Mandate lacks scope, limits, expiry and revocation.

- No retry or fallback escapes the approved envelope.

- No unknown outcome authorises blind repetition.

- No scheduled action relies on stale authority.

- No deletion or overwrite follows from ordinary write access.

- No emergency authority becomes permanent.

- No revoked authority returns through cache, queue, device or backup.

- No approval or authority failure bypasses the Intelligence Fabric.

## 24.737 Closing Statement

Approval, authority and bounded autonomy determine who may decide, who may act and how far Omnira may proceed without asking again.

Identity defines who or what is acting.

Authentication establishes confidence in that identity.

The Role describes expected responsibility.

The Authority Grant defines permitted power.

The Denial defines what remains forbidden.

The Approval Request describes the exact Decision required.

The Approval Decision binds an authorised human or governance process to an exact scope and Version.

The Approval Token carries that Decision safely to the execution boundary.

The Authority Level describes the general operating posture.

The Autonomy Envelope defines every boundary that shall constrain independent action.

The Mandate activates bounded autonomy for one objective or domain.

The Execution Authorisation proves that one exact Plan may begin or continue.

The Escalation Record preserves every deviation that requires human judgement.

The Revocation Record ensures that authority can stop.

The Emergency Stop protects the user and Project when the system must halt immediately.

The Break-Glass procedure permits exceptional recovery without converting emergency access into ordinary power.
