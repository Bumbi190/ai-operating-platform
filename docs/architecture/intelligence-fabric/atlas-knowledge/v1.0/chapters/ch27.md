# Chapter 27 — Stage 1 Implementation Architecture

<a id="section-27-1"></a>
## 27.1 Chapter Status

This chapter defines the canonical Stage 1 implementation architecture for the Omnira Intelligence Fabric.

It translates the architectural contracts established in Chapters 1 through 26 into the first deployable implementation boundary.

It establishes:

- which Intelligence Fabric components SHALL exist in Stage 1,

- which capabilities SHALL be operational before broader autonomy is introduced,

- which services SHALL be canonical,

- which functions MAY initially remain modular components inside a shared service,

- which persistence boundaries SHALL exist,

- which schemas SHALL become executable,

- how Atlas and other Agents SHALL access the Fabric,

- how Capability Requests SHALL enter the system,

- how Planning, Routing, Budgeting, Approval, Execution and Evidence SHALL connect,

- how Providers SHALL be registered and invoked,

- how local Models and local Nodes SHALL participate,

- how Credentials SHALL remain outside Agent context,

- how external and local intelligence SHALL coexist behind one capability-first interface,

- how synchronous and asynchronous work SHALL be handled,

- how MCP, APIs, queues, webhooks and manual-assisted integrations SHALL enter through governed boundaries,

- how text, code, image, Voice and multimodal workloads SHALL be represented,

- how Assets SHALL be ingested,

- how provenance SHALL be created,

- how Quality SHALL be evaluated,

- how Memory SHALL receive only governed Derived Learning,

- how Data Classification SHALL constrain routing,

- how approval SHALL bind to exact execution scope,

- how cost SHALL be estimated, reserved and reconciled,

- how logs, traces, audit and Incidents SHALL be produced,

- how developer conformance SHALL be enforced,

- which Providers and Runtime paths SHALL be implemented first,

- which features SHALL explicitly remain outside Stage 1,

- how the implementation SHALL remain migratable toward later stages,

- and what evidence SHALL prove that Stage 1 is complete.

Stage 1 is not a prototype that may ignore canonical contracts.

Stage 1 is the minimum production-capable Intelligence Fabric foundation.

Stage 1 shall prefer:

- correctness over breadth,

- explicit scope over convenience,

- bounded autonomy over unrestricted Agent freedom,

- clear interfaces over distributed complexity,

- durable evidence over hidden state,

- local and free-first options where suitable,

- Provider independence over direct SDK coupling,

- and reversible implementation choices over premature optimisation.

Stage 1 shall not attempt to implement every future feature described in this book.

Stage 1 shall implement enough of every mandatory control plane to ensure that later expansion does not require abandoning the architecture.

The implementation may begin as a modular monolith or small set of cohesive services where operational simplicity is beneficial.

Canonical boundaries SHALL exist even when process boundaries do not yet exist.

A module boundary is sufficient where distribution would create unnecessary complexity.

A database table is not a domain boundary by itself.

A microservice is not automatically better architecture.

A message bus is not required merely because future scale may require one.

A direct internal function call may be valid when:

- the canonical interface remains explicit,

- the module boundary is maintained,

- observability remains intact,

- and later extraction remains possible.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT encode Provider-specific assumptions into Atlas.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT expose raw API keys to Agents.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT allow unrestricted MCP discovery to become authority.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT allow arbitrary external Providers to be used merely because a Connector exists.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT automatically publish generated outputs.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT automatically lower Data Classification.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT automatically admit raw execution histories into permanent Memory.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT silently retry unknown external side effects.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT treat Provider acceptance as Completion.

Stage 1 SHALL NOT claim unrestricted autonomous operation.

This chapter is normative after formal approval.

Chapter 28 defines rollout, migration, anti-patterns and future evolution beyond this implementation boundary.

<a id="section-27-2"></a>
## 27.2 Stage 1 Mission

The mission of Stage 1 is to create one trustworthy Intelligence Fabric path through which Omnira may request, select, execute, evaluate and record intelligence work without depending directly on one Model, one Provider or one execution location.

Stage 1 shall prove that Omnira can:

1. receive a Capability Request.

2. classify its data and execution requirements.

3. resolve available capabilities.

4. identify eligible Models, Providers, Accounts, Tools and local Runtime resources.

5. generate an executable Plan.

6. estimate cost and resource requirements.

7. determine whether approval is required.

8. route according to policy.

9. execute through a canonical Adapter boundary.

10. handle synchronous or asynchronous results.

11. ingest resulting Assets.

12. evaluate Quality.

13. reconcile actual cost and usage.

14. preserve Execution Evidence and provenance.

15. produce governed Derived Learning where justified.

16. return a canonical Completion result to the requesting Agent or user.

<a id="section-27-3"></a>
## 27.3 Stage 1 Architectural Position

The Intelligence Fabric SHALL exist as a shared Omnira platform service.

It SHALL NOT be owned by Atlas.

Atlas SHALL be one major consumer.

Other consumers may include:

- Arnold,

- future Project Agents,

- workflow services,

- Omnira user interfaces,

- publication systems,

- test harnesses,

- and administrative control planes.

<a id="section-27-4"></a>
## 27.4 Atlas Boundary

Atlas SHALL express:

- intent,

- Project context,

- desired Capability,

- constraints,

- Quality requirements,

- Budget preferences,

- privacy requirements,

- and expected Output.

Atlas SHALL NOT need to know:

- concrete Provider API endpoints,

- API keys,

- Provider-specific request schemas,

- raw Model identifiers,

- retry headers,

- webhook formats,

- or Provider-native job state machines.

<a id="section-27-5"></a>
## 27.5 Fabric Responsibility

The Fabric SHALL determine:

- what resources are eligible,

- how work is planned,

- which resource is selected,

- how execution occurs,

- how failures are handled,

- how cost is governed,

- how evidence is produced,

- and whether Completion requirements are satisfied.

<a id="section-27-6"></a>
## 27.6 Stage 1 Completion Definition

Stage 1 is complete only when representative workflows can use the Fabric end to end without bypassing its canonical boundaries.

<a id="section-27-8"></a>
## 27.8 Stage 1 Representative Modalities

Stage 1 SHALL support at minimum:

- text,

- reasoning,

- code-oriented text generation,

- image generation,

- Voice generation,

- generic file and Asset processing,

- search or retrieval capability,

- Tool invocation,

- and local Model execution.

<a id="section-27-9"></a>
## 27.9 Stage 1 Deferred Modalities

Full production support may be deferred for:

- advanced video orchestration,

- music generation,

- fully autonomous browser production,

- complex multi-Agent marketplaces,

- custom Model training,

- large-scale fine-tuning,

- distributed GPU clusters,

- federated learning,

- and autonomous external purchasing.

The architecture SHALL leave room for them.

<a id="section-27-10"></a>
## 27.10 Stage 1 Deployment Philosophy

Stage 1 SHOULD minimise operational complexity.

The preferred initial shape is:

- one Fabric application or small cohesive service group,

- one primary relational database,

- one durable object or Asset store,

- one queue,

- one Secret system,

- one observability stack,

- and optional local Runtime Nodes.

<a id="section-27-11"></a>
## 27.11 Modular Monolith

A modular monolith is acceptable for Stage 1 when internal boundaries follow canonical domains.

<a id="section-27-12"></a>
## 27.12 Module Extraction

A module SHALL be extractable later without changing canonical contracts.

<a id="section-27-13"></a>
## 27.13 Premature Microservice Prohibition

Stage 1 SHALL NOT create separate network services merely to mirror every chapter or object.

<a id="section-27-14"></a>
## 27.14 Shared Process

The following MAY initially share one process:

- Registry,

- Planner,

- Router,

- Cost Engine,

- Policy Evaluation,

- Evidence assembly,

- and orchestration.

Their data models and interfaces SHALL remain explicit.

<a id="section-27-15"></a>
## 27.15 Separately Isolated Components

The following SHOULD have stronger process or trust isolation where practical:

- Secret resolution,

- untrusted Tool execution,

- local process execution,

- webhook ingress,

- browser-assisted operation,

- and arbitrary file processing.

<a id="section-27-17"></a>
## 27.17 Fabric API

The Fabric API is the canonical entry boundary for intelligence execution.

<a id="section-27-18"></a>
## 27.18 Fabric API Responsibilities

It SHALL:

- authenticate the caller,

- resolve Principal identity,

- resolve Tenant and Project,

- validate Intent Envelope,

- create Capability Request,

- assign correlation,

- initiate policy evaluation,

- start Planning,

- expose status,

- return Completion result,

- and expose Evidence references.

<a id="section-27-19"></a>
## 27.19 Fabric API Non-Responsibilities

It SHALL NOT:

- embed Provider SDK semantics,

- resolve raw Credentials into caller context,

- select Provider by hard-coded name,

- bypass approval,

- or fabricate synchronous completion for asynchronous work.

<a id="section-27-20"></a>
## 27.20 Stage 1 API Style

Stage 1 MAY use HTTP and JSON as the primary public internal API.

Internal module calls MAY use native language interfaces.

Canonical schemas SHALL remain transport-neutral.

<a id="section-27-21"></a>
## 27.21 Stage 1 API Operations

The first API SHOULD include operations equivalent to:

- submit Capability Request,

- inspect Request,

- inspect Plan,

- approve or deny,

- inspect Execution,

- cancel Execution,

- retrieve result,

- inspect Asset,

- inspect Evidence,

- query Registry,

- inspect Connector health,

- and inspect current Cost state.

<a id="section-27-22"></a>
## 27.22 Capability Request Submission

Request submission SHALL return:

- Request identity,

- correlation identity,

- initial state,

- whether approval may be required,

- and status location or equivalent handle.

<a id="section-27-23"></a>
## 27.23 Asynchronous API

Long-running work SHALL return a durable canonical handle rather than holding the client connection indefinitely.

<a id="section-27-24"></a>
## 27.24 Request Status

Request status SHALL remain distinct from Execution and Provider job status.

<a id="section-27-25"></a>
## 27.25 Canonical Request States

Stage 1 Capability Request states SHOULD include:

- received,

- validating,

- planning,

- awaiting approval,

- ready,

- executing,

- evaluating,

- completed,

- failed,

- cancelled,

- unknown,

- and blocked.

<a id="section-27-26"></a>
## 27.26 Intent Envelope

The Stage 1 Intent Envelope SHALL contain enough information to drive policy and planning.

<a id="section-27-27"></a>
## 27.27 Stage 1 Intent Envelope Fields

It SHOULD include or reference:

- intentEnvelopeId,

- requester Principal,

- Tenant,

- Project,

- purpose,

- Capability,

- desired Output type,

- Input references,

- Classification,

- Routing Profile,

- Quality Floor,

- maximum Budget,

- deadline,

- privacy constraints,

- preferred or prohibited resources,

- approval expectations,

- and metadata.

<a id="section-27-28"></a>
## 27.28 Intent Validation

Validation SHALL occur before Planning.

<a id="section-27-29"></a>
## 27.29 Unknown Intent Field

Unknown optional fields MAY be preserved.

Unknown mandatory semantics SHALL cause validation failure.

<a id="section-27-30"></a>
## 27.30 Request Idempotency

Request submission SHALL support idempotency for clients that may retry.

<a id="section-27-31"></a>
## 27.31 Request Deduplication

Deduplication SHALL not merge distinct human intents merely because their text is identical.

<a id="section-27-32"></a>
## 27.32 Tenant Context

Tenant context SHALL derive from trusted identity or explicit platform scope.

<a id="section-27-33"></a>
## 27.33 Project Context

Project context SHALL be mandatory for Project-scoped work.

<a id="section-27-34"></a>
## 27.34 Missing Project

Missing Project SHALL fail closed when the requested Capability uses:

- Project Memory,

- Project Assets,

- Project Credentials,

- Project Budget,

- or external Project Accounts.

<a id="section-27-35"></a>
## 27.35 Identity Service Integration

The Fabric SHALL consume canonical Principal identity rather than inventing its own authentication model.

<a id="section-27-36"></a>
## 27.36 Service Principal

Internal Fabric modules and workers SHALL use Service Principal identity.

<a id="section-27-37"></a>
## 27.37 Agent Principal

Atlas and every Agent SHALL have stable Agent Principal identity.

<a id="section-27-38"></a>
## 27.38 Resource Registry Layer

Stage 1 SHALL maintain separate canonical registries for:

- Capabilities,

- Models,

- Providers,

- Tools,

- Runtime Nodes,

- Agents,

- Provider Accounts,

- Connections,

- and Adapter Versions.

<a id="section-27-39"></a>
## 27.39 Registry Persistence

Registry records SHOULD be stored in the primary relational database initially.

<a id="section-27-40"></a>
## 27.40 Registry Read Path

Planning and Routing SHALL read through canonical Registry interfaces.

<a id="section-27-41"></a>
## 27.41 Registry Write Path

Registry mutation SHALL require authenticated administrative or governed configuration authority.

<a id="section-27-42"></a>
## 27.42 Registry Cache

Read-heavy Registry objects MAY be cached.

Cache invalidation SHALL occur on Version change.

<a id="section-27-43"></a>
## 27.43 Capability Registry Stage 1

The Capability Registry SHALL be the primary semantic discovery mechanism.

<a id="section-27-44"></a>
## 27.44 Capability Examples

Initial capability identifiers may represent:

- text.generate,

- text.reason,

- code.generate,

- image.generate,

- voice.generate,

- search.web,

- retrieval.project,

- tool.invoke,

- asset.transform,

- and model.local.generate.

<a id="section-27-45"></a>
## 27.45 Capability Hierarchy

Capabilities MAY have parent and child relationships.

Routing SHALL use exact requirements, not string-prefix assumptions alone.

<a id="section-27-46"></a>
## 27.46 Capability Requirement

A Request MAY include:

- required,

- preferred,

- prohibited,

- and optional capability features.

<a id="section-27-47"></a>
## 27.47 Capability Feature

Examples include:

- structured output,

- streaming,

- image reference input,

- Voice Profile support,

- JSON schema output,

- Tool calling,

- long context,

- or local execution.

<a id="section-27-48"></a>
## 27.48 Model Registry Stage 1

The Model Registry SHALL describe intelligence resources independently from Provider Accounts.

<a id="section-27-49"></a>
## 27.49 Model Registry Minimum Fields

- modelId,

- productId,

- native identifier,

- model family,

- Version or alias state,

- supported Capabilities,

- modalities,

- quality evidence,

- privacy properties,

## 27.50 Model Alias

Provider aliases SHALL be distinguishable from immutable Model Versions.

<a id="section-27-51"></a>
## 27.51 Alias Drift

<a id="section-27-52"></a>
## 27.52 Local Model Record

## 27.53 Local Model Provider

## 27.54 Provider Registry Stage 1

The Provider Registry SHALL represent external or local service organisations and execution systems.

<a id="section-27-55"></a>
## 27.55 Provider Minimum Fields

A Provider Record SHOULD include:

- name,

- Provider type,

- trust state,

- supported Products,

- legal or Terms references,

- privacy evidence,

- commercial-rights evidence,

- regions,

- lifecycle,

- and review date.

<a id="section-27-56"></a>
## 27.56 Provider Product

Different Products from one Provider SHALL remain distinguishable.

<a id="section-27-57"></a>
## 27.57 Provider Account Registry

Each Account SHALL identify:

- accountId,

- Provider,

- owner type,

- Tenant,

- Project scope,

- plan,

- billing mode,

- region,

- privacy settings,

- training setting,

- commercial-use status,

- and lifecycle.

## 27.58 Account Owner Type

Stage 1 SHALL support:

- user-owned,

- Tenant-owned,

- Project-owned,

- and Omnira-managed

Accounts where applicable.

<a id="section-27-59"></a>
## 27.59 User-Owned Account

User-owned Credentials SHALL remain under user authority while still being governed by Fabric policy.

<a id="section-27-60"></a>
## 27.60 Omnira-Managed Account

Omnira-managed Accounts SHALL preserve Project and Tenant attribution for cost and data use.

<a id="section-27-61"></a>
## 27.61 Connection Registry

A Connection binds:

- Provider Account,

- Credential Reference,

- Adapter Version,

- Environment,

- Project or Tenant scope,

- and allowed Capabilities.

<a id="section-27-62"></a>
## 27.62 Connection Minimum Fields

A Connection SHOULD include:

- connectionId,

- providerId,

- productId,

- accountId,

- credentialRef,

- adapterId,

- adapterVersion,

- Environment,

- Tenant,

- Project scope,

- allowed Capabilities,

- health,

- state,

- and Version.

<a id="section-27-63"></a>
## 27.63 Credential Reference

Connections SHALL store Credential References only.

<a id="section-27-64"></a>
## 27.64 Credential Resolution Boundary

Raw Secret resolution SHALL occur only at the Adapter or approved Tool execution boundary.

<a id="section-27-65"></a>
## 27.65 Credential Store

Stage 1 SHALL use an approved Secret Store.

The implementation MAY initially use:

- operating-system secure storage,

- environment-injected secure secret service,

- Vault-compatible storage,

- or another approved system.

Plaintext files SHALL NOT be canonical Credential storage.

<a id="section-27-66"></a>
## 27.66 Secret Retrieval

Secret retrieval SHALL be:

- scoped,

- auditable,

- short-lived,

- and unavailable to ordinary Agent context.

<a id="section-27-67"></a>
## 27.67 Secret Cache

If Secret values are cached in memory, the cache SHALL be:

- process-local,

- short-lived,

- non-logged,

- and cleared on revocation where practical.

<a id="section-27-68"></a>
## 27.68 Tool Registry Stage 1

The Tool Registry SHALL describe executable capabilities distinct from Models.

<a id="section-27-69"></a>
## 27.69 Tool Minimum Fields

A Tool SHOULD include:

- toolId,

- name,

- Version,

- Capability Bindings,

- input schema,

- output schema,

- side-effect class,

- authority requirements,

- Environment requirements,

- Sandbox requirements,

- and lifecycle.

<a id="section-27-70"></a>
## 27.70 MCP Tool

MCP-discovered Tools SHALL be mapped into canonical Tool Records before Agent exposure.

<a id="section-27-71"></a>
## 27.71 Runtime Node Registry

Stage 1 SHALL register local Nodes capable of intelligence or Tool execution.

<a id="section-27-72"></a>
## 27.72 Runtime Node Minimum Fields

A Node SHOULD include:

- nodeId,

- owner,

- Tenant,

- device class,

- operating system,

- hardware capabilities,

- Runtime services,

- network state,

- trust state,

- availability,

- and health.

<a id="section-27-73"></a>
## 27.73 Stage 1 Local Nodes

Representative Nodes MAY include:

- Mac laptop,

- Windows workstation,

- private AI rig,

- or future server.

<a id="section-27-74"></a>
## 27.74 Node Availability

The Scheduler SHALL not assume that personal Nodes are continuously online.

<a id="section-27-75"></a>
## 27.75 Node Start Requirement

When a required Local Only Node is offline, the Fabric SHALL:

- wait,

- notify,

- or request the user to start it

rather than violating placement policy.

<a id="section-27-76"></a>
## 27.76 Policy Engine

Stage 1 SHALL implement one central policy evaluation layer.

<a id="section-27-77"></a>
## 27.77 Policy Domains

- Data Classification,

- Provider eligibility,

- Routing constraints,

- Budget,

- authority,

- approval,

- rights,

- consent,

- local placement,

- and publication restrictions.

<a id="section-27-78"></a>
## 27.78 Policy Result

Policy evaluation SHALL return:

- permitted,

- permitted with conditions,

- denied,

- approval required,

- or indeterminate.

<a id="section-27-79"></a>
## 27.79 Indeterminate Policy

Indeterminate SHALL fail closed for consequential actions.

<a id="section-27-80"></a>
## 27.80 Policy Version

Every Decision SHALL reference applicable policy Versions.

<a id="section-27-81"></a>
## 27.81 Policy Representation

Stage 1 MAY represent policy through:

- typed application rules,

- configuration,

- or a policy engine.

Human-readable canonical policy SHALL remain authoritative.

<a id="section-27-82"></a>
## 27.82 Premature Policy Language

A full general-purpose policy DSL is not required for Stage 1.

<a id="section-27-83"></a>
## 27.83 Classification Engine

Stage 1 SHALL classify every material Input and Asset.

<a id="section-27-84"></a>
## 27.84 Classification Levels

The five canonical levels SHALL be implemented:

- Public,

- Project Internal,

- Confidential,

- Local Only,

- Prohibited.

<a id="section-27-85"></a>
## 27.85 Classification Default

Unknown sensitive data SHALL receive restrictive treatment.

<a id="section-27-86"></a>
## 27.86 Automatic Classifier

An automatic classifier MAY propose:

- Data Category,

- Classification,

- confidence,

- and reasons.

<a id="section-27-87"></a>
## 27.87 User Tightening

Users SHALL be able to tighten Classification.

<a id="section-27-88"></a>
## 27.88 Automatic Lowering

Automatic lowering SHALL be prohibited.

<a id="section-27-89"></a>
## 27.89 Classification Propagation

Classification SHALL propagate to:

- prompts,

- Provider Inputs,

- generated Outputs,

- Assets,

- embeddings,

- Evidence,

- and Memory proposals.

<a id="section-27-90"></a>
## 27.90 Project Isolation

Every persisted object SHALL include Tenant and Project scope where applicable.

<a id="section-27-91"></a>
## 27.91 Database Enforcement

Stage 1 SHOULD use structural access enforcement such as:

- scoped repository methods,

- row-level security,

- separate schemas,

- or equivalent controls.

Application filters alone SHALL NOT be the only high-risk isolation mechanism.

<a id="section-27-92"></a>
## 27.92 Object Storage Isolation

Asset keys SHALL include canonical scope and shall not depend solely on human-readable filenames.

<a id="section-27-93"></a>
## 27.93 Queue Isolation

Queue messages SHALL preserve Tenant and Project identity.

<a id="section-27-94"></a>
## 27.94 Cache Isolation

Cache keys SHALL include Tenant and Project where data or Decisions are scoped.

<a id="section-27-95"></a>
## 27.95 Search Isolation

Search and vector retrieval SHALL apply access eligibility before similarity ranking.

<a id="section-27-96"></a>
## 27.96 Planner

The Planner SHALL convert one Capability Request into one Versioned Execution Plan.

<a id="section-27-97"></a>
## 27.97 Planner Input

The Planner SHALL receive:

- Intent Envelope,

- available Capabilities,

- policy constraints,

- Budget,

- Routing Profile,

- Quality requirements,

- data placement,

- deadline,

- and resource health.

<a id="section-27-98"></a>
## 27.98 Planner Output

The Planner SHALL produce:

- Plan identity,

- Plan Version,

- Steps,

- dependencies,

- required Capabilities,

- expected Inputs and Outputs,

- resource constraints,

- approval gates,

- Budget allocation,

- fallback policy,

- completion criteria,

- and evidence requirements.

<a id="section-27-99"></a>
## 27.99 Plan Persistence

Every executable Plan SHALL be stored durably before execution.

<a id="section-27-100"></a>
## 27.100 Plan Immutability

An approved Plan Version SHALL be immutable.

Replanning creates a new Version.

<a id="section-27-101"></a>
## 27.101 Plan Step

Every Step SHALL have:

- stepId,

- operation,

- Capability,

- Inputs,

- expected Outputs,

- dependency list,

- side-effect class,

- retry policy,

- Quality requirements,

- Budget,

- and state.

<a id="section-27-102"></a>
## 27.102 Step State

Canonical Stage 1 Step states SHOULD include:

- pending,

- ready,

- blocked,

- queued,

- running,

- awaiting external completion,

- awaiting human,

- evaluating,

- succeeded,

- failed,

- cancelled,

- skipped,

- unknown,

- and reconciliation required.

<a id="section-27-103"></a>
## 27.103 Execution Graph

The Plan SHALL be represented as a directed execution graph.

<a id="section-27-104"></a>
## 27.104 Acyclic Default

Stage 1 SHOULD prefer acyclic execution graphs.

Loops MAY be represented through bounded retry or explicit iteration constructs.

<a id="section-27-105"></a>
## 27.105 Cycle Prohibition

Unbounded graph cycles SHALL be rejected.

<a id="section-27-106"></a>
## 27.106 Completion Contract

Every Plan SHALL define Completion conditions.

<a id="section-27-107"></a>

## 27.810 Closing Statement

Stage 1 is where the Omnira Intelligence Fabric stops being architecture alone and becomes the governed execution foundation of the platform.

The Capability Request gives Agents a Provider-independent way to ask for intelligence.

The Intent Envelope gives the Fabric enough context to understand purpose, scope, privacy, Quality, Budget and constraints.

The Registries describe what intelligence resources exist.

The Policy Engine determines what is allowed.

The Planner determines what work must occur.

The Cost Engine determines what that work may cost.

The Router determines which eligible resources should perform it.

The Approval Control Plane determines whether consequential execution may proceed.

The Execution Orchestrator turns the approved Plan into durable work.

The Queue and Worker layer make long-running and failure-prone operations recoverable.

The Adapter layer translates canonical intelligence into Provider-native operations.

The Connector layer binds those Adapters to exact Accounts, Credentials, Environments and Projects.

The Async Job Manager preserves external work beyond one process or network connection.

The Webhook and Polling paths reconcile distributed Provider state.

The Local Runtime Manager gives local Models first-class status without making them governance exceptions.

The MCP client makes Tool ecosystems available without allowing protocol discovery to become authority.

The Asset Service turns temporary external results into durable Omnira objects.

The Quality Service determines whether those outputs are actually usable.

The Evidence Service preserves how each material result came into existence.

The Memory Admission Boundary ensures that execution history does not silently become permanent belief.

The Classification and Privacy controls determine where data may travel.

The Authority controls determine who may cause consequential actions.

The Observability layer makes the execution understandable.

The Audit layer makes it accountable.

The Incident layer makes failures containable and recoverable.

The Conformance layer proves that implementations match the architecture.
