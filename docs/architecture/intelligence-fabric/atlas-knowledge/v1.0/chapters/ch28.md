# Chapter 28 — Rollout, Migration, Anti-Patterns and Future Evolution

<a id="section-28-1"></a>
## 28.1 Chapter Status

This chapter defines the canonical rollout, migration, adoption, transition, deprecation, anti-pattern and future-evolution architecture for the Omnira Intelligence Fabric.

It establishes:

- how the Fabric SHALL move from architecture into operational use,

- how existing direct Agent-to-Provider paths SHALL be inventoried,

- how legacy Provider integrations SHALL migrate behind the Fabric boundary,

- how Credentials SHALL migrate without unnecessary Secret exposure,

- how Provider Accounts and Connections SHALL be canonicalised,

- how existing Models, Tools, local Runtimes and MCP servers SHALL enter the Registries,

- how legacy workflows SHALL migrate without losing operational continuity,

- how Assets SHALL be imported without fabricated provenance,

- how historical execution data SHALL be represented when canonical evidence is incomplete,

- how existing Memory SHALL be preserved without pretending that it originated through the new governance model,

- how current Project behaviour SHALL remain stable while infrastructure changes underneath it,

- how Atlas and other Agents SHALL transition away from direct Provider knowledge,

- how compatibility wrappers SHALL remain temporary,

- how shadow mode, dual execution, canaries and phased routing SHALL reduce migration risk,

- how Stage 1 SHALL be introduced without requiring a complete platform rewrite,

- how migration SHALL remain reversible until verification is complete,

- how users and Project owners SHALL retain control throughout transition,

- how data, cost, privacy, rights, consent and authority SHALL remain enforced during migration,

- how observability SHALL determine rollout health,

- how rollback and rollforward SHALL operate,

- how drift between legacy and canonical systems SHALL be detected,

- how deprecated integration paths SHALL be retired,

- how direct Provider bypass SHALL be eliminated gradually but decisively,

- how rollout SHALL differ across Atlas, Familje-Stunden, The Prompt, GainPilot and future Projects,

- how anti-patterns SHALL be identified before they become permanent architecture,

- how emergency exceptions SHALL avoid becoming permanent technical debt,

- how later stages SHALL evolve from Stage 1 without invalidating canonical identities or contracts,

- how the Fabric MAY scale into distributed execution, richer multimodal orchestration, advanced local intelligence, continuous benchmarking and broader Agent autonomy,

- how future standards, Providers and protocols SHALL be absorbed through canonical interfaces,

- how architectural decisions SHALL be revisited without rewriting history,

- how deprecation SHALL remain visible and time-bounded,

- how future intelligence improvements SHALL remain subordinate to policy, privacy, authority and user intent,

- and how Omnira SHALL preserve the doctrine of No Intelligence Without Fabric as the platform evolves.

Migration is not a file move alone.

Rollout is not deployment alone.

A compatibility layer is not completion.

A legacy workflow that still requires a direct Provider SDK is not fully migrated.

A Provider Connection copied into a new database is not canonicalisation unless scope, Account, Credential, policy and evidence are correct.

An imported Asset does not gain provenance that never existed.

An old Model interaction does not become canonical Execution Evidence retroactively.

A new routing layer does not justify breaking Project behaviour.

A successful canary does not prove universal readiness.

A shadow Decision does not create authority.

A dual-run Output does not automatically become production Output.

A rollback shall not restore revoked authority, deleted data or expired Consent.

A migration shall not resurrect obsolete Credentials.

A feature flag shall not become a permanent hidden fork.

A temporary Provider bypass shall not become a second architecture.

A future protocol shall not bypass canonical policy because it is fashionable.

A new Agent framework shall not become the owner of Agent identity.

A new Model router shall not become the owner of routing policy.

A new vector database shall not become the owner of Memory semantics.

A new orchestration engine shall not become the owner of Plan semantics.

A new multimodal Provider shall not become the owner of Asset identity.

A new local Runtime shall not become a privacy exception.

A stronger Model shall not receive broader authority automatically.

A cheaper Provider shall not lower Data Classification.

A faster workflow shall not remove provenance.

An autonomous workflow shall not remove user stop authority.

Future evolution SHALL extend the Fabric.

It SHALL NOT route around it.

This chapter is normative after formal approval.

It closes the canonical first edition of the Omnira Intelligence Fabric architecture.

Implementation sequencing, delivery planning and repository-specific execution SHALL be derived from this book without weakening its contracts.

<a id="section-28-2"></a>
## 28.2 Purpose of Rollout and Migration

The Intelligence Fabric enters an existing Omnira ecosystem.

Existing systems may already contain:

- direct Model calls,

- Provider SDK integrations,

- manually stored API keys,

- Agent-specific Provider configuration,

- Project-specific scripts,

- local Model commands,

- browser-assisted workflows,

- manual creative workflows,

- publication routines,

- Asset folders,

- Memory records,

- scheduling logic,

- retry logic,

- cost assumptions,

- and external Accounts.

The migration architecture exists to move those systems into one governed Fabric without:

- interrupting valuable Project work unnecessarily,

- losing data,

- fabricating historical evidence,

- expanding access,

- changing user-visible behaviour accidentally,

- hiding cost,

- weakening privacy,

- or creating permanent compatibility debt.

<a id="section-28-3"></a>
## 28.3 Migration Objective

The primary migration objective is:

Every production intelligence operation SHALL eventually enter through a canonical Capability Request and execute through governed Fabric resources.

<a id="section-28-4"></a>
## 28.4 Migration Unit

The preferred migration unit is one coherent workflow or Capability path.

Examples include:

- Atlas text reasoning,

- The Prompt research,

- Familje-Stunden image generation,

- Familje-Stunden Voice narration,

- GainPilot coaching reasoning,

- local Model inference,

- publication,

- or MCP Tool invocation.

<a id="section-28-5"></a>
## 28.5 Migration Is Incremental

The Fabric SHALL support incremental migration.

A complete platform cutover in one event is not required.

<a id="section-28-6"></a>
## 28.6 Migration Is Governed

Every migration SHALL have:

- scope,

- owner,

- source state,

- target state,

- risks,

- data impact,

- authority impact,

- rollback,

- verification,

- and completion criteria.

<a id="section-28-7"></a>
## 28.7 Migration Plan

A Migration Plan is the canonical object describing transition from one implementation state to another.

It SHALL include or reference:

- migrationPlanId,

- objective,

- owner,

- source architecture,

- target architecture,

- affected Projects,

- affected Capabilities,

- affected Agents,

- affected Providers,

- affected Accounts,

- affected Credentials,

- data objects,

- dependencies,

- migration phases,

- verification,

- rollback,

- deadline,

- and state.

<a id="section-28-8"></a>
## 28.8 Migration Plan Identity

Every material Migration Plan SHALL have stable identity and Version.

<a id="section-28-9"></a>
## 28.9 Migration State

Canonical Migration Plan states MAY include:

- proposed,

- approved,

- inventorying,

- preparing,

- shadowing,

- canary,

- migrating,

- verifying,

- partially migrated,

- completed,

- paused,

- rolled back,

- failed,

- superseded,

- and archived.

<a id="section-28-10"></a>
## 28.10 Migration Owner

Every migration SHALL have one accountable owner.

<a id="section-28-11"></a>
## 28.11 Migration Scope

Scope SHALL identify exact:

- Tenant,

- Project,

- repository,

- service,

- Agent,

- Capability,

- Provider path,

- Account,

- data,

- and Environment.

<a id="section-28-12"></a>
## 28.12 Migration Boundary

Unrelated systems SHALL remain outside the migration unless explicitly included.

<a id="section-28-13"></a>
## 28.13 Migration Dependency

Dependencies SHALL be identified before cutover.

<a id="section-28-14"></a>
## 28.14 Migration Precondition

A migration SHALL not begin consequential cutover until required target controls exist.

<a id="section-28-15"></a>
## 28.15 Migration Postcondition

A migration is complete only when defined target invariants are verified.

<a id="section-28-16"></a>
## 28.16 Migration Evidence

Every material migration SHALL produce evidence of:

- source state,

- changes,

- target state,

- tests,

- data checks,

- policy checks,

- rollback readiness,

- and completion.

<a id="section-28-17"></a>
## 28.17 Migration Record

A Migration Record SHALL preserve actual transition history.

It SHALL remain distinct from the Migration Plan.

<a id="section-28-18"></a>
## 28.18 Migration Record Identity

Every material Migration Record SHALL have stable identity.

<a id="section-28-19"></a>
## 28.19 Source Architecture Inventory

Before migration, Omnira SHALL identify existing intelligence paths.

<a id="section-28-20"></a>
## 28.20 Inventory Scope

The inventory SHOULD capture:

- caller,

- Agent,

- Project,

- Capability,

- Provider,

- Product,

- Model,

- SDK,

- endpoint,

- Credential source,

- Account,

- Input data,

- Output type,

- cost,

- retry logic,

- fallback logic,

- side effects,

- Memory use,

- publication,

- and observability.

<a id="section-28-21"></a>
## 28.21 Direct Provider Call

A Direct Provider Call is any intelligence call that bypasses the Fabric’s canonical Adapter and Routing architecture.

<a id="section-28-22"></a>
## 28.22 Direct Provider Call Registry

During migration, known direct calls SHOULD be registered as migration debt.

<a id="section-28-23"></a>
## 28.23 Migration Debt

Migration Debt is a known legacy path not yet fully governed by the Fabric.

<a id="section-28-24"></a>
## 28.24 Migration Debt Identity

Every material Migration Debt item SHOULD have stable identity.

<a id="section-28-25"></a>
## 28.25 Migration Debt Fields

It SHOULD include:

- debtId,

- source,

- Project,

- Capability,

- risk,

- owner,

- temporary controls,

- target migration,

- due date,

- and state.

<a id="section-28-26"></a>
## 28.26 Migration Debt State

States MAY include:

- identified,

- controlled,

- in migration,

- blocked,

- resolved,

- accepted temporarily,

- and expired.

<a id="section-28-27"></a>
## 28.27 Migration Debt Priority

Priority SHOULD increase with:

- sensitive data,

- high spend,

- broad authority,

- external side effects,

- publication,

- private Accounts,

- or unstable Provider dependencies.

<a id="section-28-28"></a>
## 28.28 High-Priority Legacy Paths

The first migrations SHOULD include:

- Atlas core reasoning,

- Project-sensitive reasoning,

- paid multimodal generation,

- publication paths,

- external message paths,

- and workflows with weak Credential handling.

<a id="section-28-29"></a>
## 28.29 Low-Priority Legacy Paths

Low-risk internal experiments MAY remain temporarily outside production migration scope when they are:

- clearly non-production,

- isolated,

- non-sensitive,

- non-consequential,

- and time-bounded.

<a id="section-28-30"></a>
## 28.30 Inventory Completeness

Unknown legacy integrations SHALL remain a migration risk.

<a id="section-28-31"></a>
## 28.31 Repository Search

Code search SHOULD identify:

- Provider SDK imports,

- direct HTTP endpoints,

- API key names,

- Model names,

- Tool subprocesses,

- MCP configuration,

- and publication SDKs.

<a id="section-28-32"></a>
## 28.32 Configuration Search

Configuration SHOULD be inspected for:

- Provider endpoints,

- Account IDs,

- Model aliases,

- embedded Credentials,

- and direct routing flags.

<a id="section-28-33"></a>
## 28.33 Runtime Observation

Observability SHOULD identify intelligence traffic not represented in the inventory.

<a id="section-28-34"></a>
## 28.34 Cost Observation

Provider billing MAY reveal hidden legacy call paths.

<a id="section-28-35"></a>
## 28.35 Network Observation

Controlled egress telemetry MAY reveal direct Provider access outside Adapters.

<a id="section-28-36"></a>
## 28.36 Migration Discovery Shall Be Non-Destructive

Discovery SHALL prefer read-only inspection.

<a id="section-28-37"></a>
## 28.37 No Secret Dump for Inventory

Credential discovery SHALL record location and reference.

It SHALL NOT dump Secret values into migration reports.

<a id="section-28-38"></a>
## 28.38 Legacy Credential Inventory

Existing Credentials SHALL be classified by:

- Provider,

- Account,

- owner,

- Project use,

- Environment,

- storage location,

- scope,

- age,

- and status.

<a id="section-28-39"></a>
## 28.39 Unknown Credential Ownership

Credentials with uncertain owner or Project SHALL not become active Fabric Connections automatically.

<a id="section-28-40"></a>
## 28.40 Credential Canonicalisation

A legacy Credential is canonicalised only when:

- owner is identified,

- Account is identified,

- scope is defined,

- Secret is stored in approved storage,

- Connection is registered,

- and old exposure paths are retired.

<a id="section-28-41"></a>
## 28.41 Credential Rotation During Migration

High-risk legacy Credentials SHOULD be rotated when moved into the Fabric.

<a id="section-28-42"></a>
## 28.42 Credential Reuse

A Credential MAY remain the same Secret during low-risk migration if safe.

Its storage and access model SHALL still change.

<a id="section-28-43"></a>
## 28.43 Credential Exposure Review

If legacy code embedded Secrets in:

- source control,

- logs,

- documentation,

- chat,

- or scripts,

rotation SHALL be considered mandatory.

<a id="section-28-44"></a>
## 28.44 Credential History

Historical Secret values SHALL not be copied into canonical Evidence.

<a id="section-28-45"></a>
## 28.45 Legacy Provider Account Inventory

Existing Accounts SHALL be mapped to canonical Provider Account records.

<a id="section-28-46"></a>
## 28.46 Account Ownership Verification

Migration SHALL verify whether each Account is:

- personal,

- Project-owned,

- Tenant-owned,

- organisation-owned,

- or Omnira-managed.

<a id="section-28-47"></a>
## 28.47 Account Terms Review

Material Accounts SHOULD be reviewed for:

- billing,

- retention,

- training settings,

- commercial rights,

- region,

- and usage policy.

<a id="section-28-48"></a>
## 28.48 Account Scope Migration

One legacy Account used by several Projects SHALL not automatically become a shared unrestricted Connection.

<a id="section-28-49"></a>
## 28.49 Shared Account

A shared Account MAY remain shared if policy permits.

Each Project’s Connection and authority SHALL remain explicit.

<a id="section-28-50"></a>
## 28.50 Provider Registry Migration

Legacy Provider definitions SHALL be mapped into the Provider Registry.

<a id="section-28-51"></a>
## 28.51 Model Registry Migration

Known Models SHALL be registered with:

- native identifier,

- family,

- Provider,

- Capability evidence,

- and lifecycle state.

<a id="section-28-52"></a>
## 28.52 Unknown Model Version

Historical aliases whose exact underlying Version is unknown SHALL be recorded as unresolved historical identity.

<a id="section-28-53"></a>
## 28.53 No Fabricated Model Version

Migration SHALL NOT invent immutable Model identity where evidence does not exist.

<a id="section-28-54"></a>
## 28.54 Tool Inventory

Legacy Tools SHALL be mapped by:

- Tool identity,

- command,

- schema,

- file scope,

- network scope,

- side effect,

- authority,

- and Project.

<a id="section-28-55"></a>
## 28.55 Unregistered Tool

A legacy Tool SHALL not become production-eligible until it passes Tool conformance.

<a id="section-28-56"></a>
## 28.56 Local Runtime Inventory

Existing local AI Runtimes SHALL be inventoried.

<a id="section-28-57"></a>
## 28.57 Local Runtime Properties

Inventory SHOULD capture:

- Node,

- Runtime,

- installed Models,

- hardware,

- network behaviour,

- storage,

- file permissions,

- and launch method.

<a id="section-28-58"></a>
## 28.58 Local Runtime Canonicalisation

Local execution becomes canonical when it uses:

- Node identity,

- Runtime identity,

- Model Registry,

- canonical Attempt,

- policy,

- and Evidence.

<a id="section-28-59"></a>
## 28.59 Legacy Shell Command

A direct shell call to a local Model MAY be wrapped temporarily.

It SHALL eventually migrate behind a local Runtime Adapter or Tool contract.

<a id="section-28-60"></a>
## 28.60 MCP Inventory

Existing MCP servers SHALL be inventoried by:

- server identity,

- transport,

- package,

- Version,

- Tools,

- Resources,

- Prompts,

- file access,

- network access,

- Credentials,

- and trust.

<a id="section-28-61"></a>
## 28.61 MCP Migration

A legacy MCP server SHALL not become Agent-accessible through the Fabric until:

- server identity is registered,

- discovery snapshot exists,

- schemas validate,

- Tools are classified,

- scope is approved,

- and conformance passes.

<a id="section-28-62"></a>
## 28.62 Agent Inventory

Agents SHALL be inventoried by:

- identity,

- Project,

- role,

- current Models,

- Providers,

- Tools,

- Memory,

- authority,

- and direct integrations.

<a id="section-28-63"></a>
## 28.63 Agent Identity Preservation

Migration SHALL preserve Agent identity even when all underlying Models or Providers change.

<a id="section-28-64"></a>
## 28.64 Agent Provider Decoupling

Direct Provider references SHALL move from Agent configuration into Fabric resource and routing policy.

<a id="section-28-65"></a>
## 28.65 Agent Capability Mapping

Legacy Agent functions SHOULD be mapped to canonical Capabilities.

<a id="section-28-66"></a>
## 28.66 Atlas Migration

Atlas SHALL migrate from direct intelligence implementation knowledge toward Capability Requests.

<a id="section-28-67"></a>
## 28.67 Atlas Migration Principle

Atlas SHOULD retain:

- strategic role,

- context,

- intent,

- coordination,

- and user interaction

while losing:

- API key knowledge,

- endpoint knowledge,

- SDK coupling,

- and raw Provider routing logic.

<a id="section-28-68"></a>
## 28.68 Compatibility Adapter for Atlas

A temporary Atlas compatibility interface MAY translate existing calls into Capability Requests.

<a id="section-28-69"></a>
## 28.69 Atlas Compatibility Wrapper

The wrapper SHALL:

- preserve current call shape where necessary,

- create canonical Request,

- attach Project,

- attach classification,

- attach Budget,

- and return canonical result.

<a id="section-28-70"></a>
## 28.70 Atlas Compatibility Wrapper Expiry

The wrapper SHALL have an owner and retirement criterion.

<a id="section-28-71"></a>
## 28.71 No New Direct Atlas Integration

After Fabric migration begins, new Atlas Provider integrations SHALL be prohibited outside the Fabric.

<a id="section-28-72"></a>
## 28.72 Project Agent Migration

Project Agents SHALL migrate independently by Capability path.

<a id="section-28-73"></a>
## 28.73 Arnold Migration

Arnold SHOULD migrate reasoning, local intelligence and Tool use through the Fabric without changing Arnold’s canonical coaching identity.

<a id="section-28-74"></a>
## 28.74 Workflow Inventory

Legacy workflows SHALL be inventoried by:

- trigger,

- Steps,

- Provider calls,

- human Steps,

- retries,

- state,

- side effects,

- publication,

- and Completion.

<a id="section-28-75"></a>
## 28.75 Workflow Canonicalisation

A workflow becomes Fabric-governed when:

- it creates canonical Requests or Plans,

- every intelligence call uses registered resources,

- external side effects use Connectors,

- cost is recorded,

- authority is enforced,

- and Evidence exists.

<a id="section-28-76"></a>
## 28.76 Partial Workflow Migration

A workflow MAY be partially migrated.

The boundary between canonical and legacy Steps SHALL be explicit.

<a id="section-28-77"></a>
## 28.77 Legacy Step

A Legacy Step is a workflow Step still executed by pre-Fabric infrastructure.

<a id="section-28-78"></a>
## 28.78 Legacy Step Evidence

Legacy Steps SHALL be labelled as legacy and may have incomplete Evidence.

<a id="section-28-79"></a>
## 28.79 Legacy Step Risk

Legacy Steps with:

- payment,

- publication,

- deletion,

- sensitive data,

- or private Accounts

SHOULD be migrated early.

<a id="section-28-80"></a>
## 28.80 Legacy Workflow State

Migration SHALL preserve active workflow state where feasible.

<a id="section-28-81"></a>
## 28.81 In-Flight Workflow Cutover

In-flight work SHOULD generally finish on its original execution path unless:

- active risk requires migration,

- the Provider path is unsafe,

- or the state can be transferred without ambiguity.

<a id="section-28-82"></a>
## 28.82 Mid-Execution Migration

Moving a live external job between orchestration systems SHALL require explicit reconciliation.

<a id="section-28-83"></a>
## 28.83 No Duplicate Execution During Cutover

Cutover SHALL prevent both legacy and Fabric systems from executing the same consequential Step independently.

<a id="section-28-84"></a>
## 28.84 Execution Ownership

Every in-flight Step SHALL have one canonical execution owner.

<a id="section-28-85"></a>
## 28.85 Ownership Transfer

If ownership transfers, the handoff SHALL preserve:

- current state,

- external IDs,

- idempotency,

- authority,

- and Evidence.

<a id="section-28-86"></a>
## 28.86 Shadow Mode

Shadow Mode allows the Fabric to compute:

- Plan,

- Routing Decision,

- cost estimate,

- policy result,

- or evaluation

without controlling production execution.

<a id="section-28-87"></a>
## 28.87 Shadow Purpose

Shadow Mode SHALL be used to compare new decisions with current behaviour safely.

<a id="section-28-88"></a>
## 28.88 Shadow No-Side-Effect

Shadow execution SHALL not create external side effects unless separately authorised as a test.

<a id="section-28-89"></a>
## 28.89 Shadow Routing

Shadow Routing MAY compare which Provider the Fabric would select against the legacy Provider choice.

<a id="section-28-90"></a>
## 28.90 Shadow Cost

Shadow Cost MAY compare predicted cost with actual legacy cost.

<a id="section-28-91"></a>
## 28.91 Shadow Policy

Shadow Policy MAY identify legacy operations that the new Fabric would block.

<a id="section-28-92"></a>
## 28.92 Shadow Authority

Shadow authority evaluation SHALL NOT create execution permission.

<a id="section-28-93"></a>
## 28.93 Shadow Evidence

Shadow results SHALL be labelled non-authoritative.

<a id="section-28-94"></a>
## 28.94 Shadow Success

Shadow agreement is useful evidence but is not required when the legacy system itself is unsafe or wrong.

<a id="section-28-95"></a>
## 28.95 Dual Run

Dual Run executes the same non-consequential workload through legacy and Fabric paths for comparison.

<a id="section-28-96"></a>
## 28.96 Dual Run Scope

Dual Run SHOULD use:

- read-only,

- synthetic,

- draft,

- or non-public work

unless duplicate side effects are impossible.

<a id="section-28-97"></a>
## 28.97 Dual Run Cost

Dual Run SHALL account for duplicate cost.

<a id="section-28-98"></a>
## 28.98 Dual Run Data

Both execution paths SHALL independently satisfy data eligibility.

<a id="section-28-99"></a>
## 28.99 Dual Run Result

Results MAY be compared for:

- Quality,

- latency,

- cost,

- correctness,

- and provenance.

<a id="section-28-100"></a>
## 28.100 Dual Run Publication Prohibition

Public publication SHALL NOT normally execute through both paths.

<a id="section-28-101"></a>
## 28.101 Canary Migration

Canary Migration routes a small bounded subset of production traffic through the Fabric.

<a id="section-28-102"></a>
## 28.102 Canary Scope

Canary scope MAY be defined by:

- Project,

- user,

- Capability,

- request class,

- data class,

- Provider,

- percentage,

- or time window.

<a id="section-28-103"></a>
## 28.103 Canary Eligibility

High-risk data SHOULD not be the first canary unless the controls being validated specifically concern that risk and approval exists.

<a id="section-28-104"></a>
## 28.104 Canary Success Metrics

Canary evaluation SHOULD include:

- completion,

- latency,

- Quality,

- cost,

- retry,

- unknown outcome,

- policy denial,

- user correction,

- and Incident rate.

<a id="section-28-105"></a>
## 28.105 Canary Abort Threshold

Abort criteria SHALL be defined before rollout.

<a id="section-28-106"></a>
## 28.106 Canary Expansion

Canary scope MAY expand only after evidence supports it.

<a id="section-28-107"></a>
## 28.107 Percentage Rollout

Percentage-based rollout SHALL preserve deterministic assignment where comparison matters.

<a id="section-28-108"></a>
## 28.108 Project-Based Rollout

Project-based migration MAY be preferable when Projects have materially different policies.

<a id="section-28-109"></a>
## 28.109 Capability-Based Rollout

Capability-based migration MAY be preferable when one shared service provides the same Capability to several Projects.

<a id="section-28-110"></a>
## 28.110 Provider-Based Rollout

Provider-based migration MAY migrate one Adapter at a time.

<a id="section-28-111"></a>
## 28.111 Agent-Based Rollout

Agent-based migration MAY migrate Atlas before Arnold or vice versa according to risk and dependency.

<a id="section-28-112"></a>
## 28.112 Feature Flag Rollout

Feature flags MAY control Fabric adoption during migration.

<a id="section-28-113"></a>
## 28.113 Feature Flag Identity

Every migration-relevant feature flag SHALL have stable identity and owner.

<a id="section-28-114"></a>
## 28.114 Feature Flag Scope

Flags SHALL identify exact:

- Environment,

- Tenant,

- Project,

- Capability,

- and user group

where applicable.

<a id="section-28-115"></a>
## 28.115 Feature Flag Expiry

Migration flags SHALL have cleanup criteria.

<a id="section-28-116"></a>
## 28.116 Permanent Flag Prohibition

A migration flag SHALL not remain permanently because no one wants to remove legacy code.

<a id="section-28-117"></a>
## 28.117 Cutover

Cutover makes the Fabric path authoritative for a defined scope.

<a id="section-28-118"></a>
## 28.118 Cutover Preconditions

Before cutover:

- Fabric path passes conformance,

- observability is active,

- rollback is possible,

- source state is understood,

- data and authority are verified,

- and owners are informed.

<a id="section-28-119"></a>
## 28.119 Cutover Window

A planned cutover MAY use a defined operational window.

<a id="section-28-120"></a>
## 28.120 Cutover Freeze

High-risk migrations MAY briefly freeze mutation to prevent conflicting state.

<a id="section-28-121"></a>
## 28.121 Cutover Verification

Immediately after cutover, the team SHALL verify:

- request flow,

- routing,

- Provider dispatch,

- result handling,

- cost,

- Evidence,

- and user-facing behaviour.

<a id="section-28-122"></a>
## 28.122 Post-Cutover Monitoring

Enhanced monitoring SHOULD remain active after cutover.

<a id="section-28-123"></a>
## 28.123 Rollback

Rollback restores the previous operational path when the new path is unsafe or materially defective.

- revocation,
